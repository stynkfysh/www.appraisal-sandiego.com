Property Tax Appeal Appraisal

Evidence of January 1 market value for a San Diego County assessment appeal.

Property tax bills in California are built on assessed value, and assessed value is not always market value. If prices have dipped, your home has problems the Assessor does not know about, or a new assessment after a purchase or construction looks too high, you can ask for a review. What you need is evidence. Brian Ward prepares appraisals for San Diego County property tax reviews and appeals, valued as of the January 1 lien date and supported by sales a hearing board can follow.

Who should consider a tax appeal appraisal

Owners who bought near a market peak are frequent candidates, since a purchase sets the base year value and a later drop can leave the assessment above what the home would sell for. Owners whose homes have physical issues, such as slope movement, a failing septic system or unrepaired damage, may also be over-assessed. New construction and major remodels get assessed too, and those added values are worth checking. Tax consultants and attorneys who handle appeals order appraisals for clients when the amount at stake justifies a formal hearing.

Prop 13, Prop 8 and the lien date

Under Proposition 13, your assessed value starts at the purchase price or the value of new construction, then can rise by no more than 2 percent a year. Proposition 8 adds a safety valve: if market value on January 1 falls below that factored base year value, the Assessor should enroll the lower market value for that year. Once the market recovers, the assessment can climb back toward the factored base, even faster than 2 percent a year.

That makes January 1 the date that matters. For an appeal, the appraisal has to set the property's value on the lien date in the disputed tax year, based on sales that closed near that date. In San Diego County, the regular filing period for a formal appeal with the Assessment Appeals Board generally runs from July 2 through November 30, and supplemental or escape assessments have their own shorter deadlines. Confirm current dates with the Clerk of the Board or your tax advisor.

How Brian handles appeals in San Diego County

Many owners start with an informal decline-in-value review by the Assessor, which costs nothing to request. If that does not resolve it, a formal appeal goes to a hearing where your evidence is weighed against the Assessor's. Brian chooses comparable sales that closed close to the lien date, prefers the ones nearest in location and features, and explains adjustments in plain terms. In places where values vary sharply street by street, such as canyon-rim lots in Mission Hills or view and non-view homes in Rancho Penasquitos, that local judgment is what makes the evidence persuasive.

Keep the appraisal focused on the right question. The board is not asking what you paid, what a neighbor's tax bill is, or whether the tax feels fair. The question is what the home would have brought on the market as of January 1. A report built tightly around that question tends to get a better hearing than a stack of loosely related listings. Brian keeps the analysis on that single point from start to finish.

Prop 19 base-year transfers

Some owners need a value for a different property tax reason. Proposition 19 lets homeowners who are 55 or older, severely disabled, or whose home was destroyed by a wildfire or natural disaster transfer their taxable value to a replacement home anywhere in California. If the replacement costs more than the original home's market value, part of the difference is added to the transferred base. Values on both sides of that comparison matter, and an appraisal can document the original home's value at the time of sale. Ask your tax advisor how the rules apply to you.

Questions about this appraisal

Is it worth paying for an appraisal to lower my taxes?

Do the math first. Compare the assessed value on your notice with what you believe the home was worth on January 1, then multiply the difference by roughly 1 percent plus local assessments to estimate the yearly savings. If the gap is small, an informal review with your own sales research may be enough. If it is large, or the Assessor declined your informal request, a formal appraisal can pay for itself.

Does the appraisal have to be as of January 1?

For a regular assessment appeal, yes. The question before the board is market value on the lien date, so the appraisal must use that effective date and sales from around that time. A current-date appraisal can be off in either direction if the market moved since. When a purchase or new construction triggers a supplemental assessment, the date of that event is used instead.

Will you attend the appeal hearing?

Brian can appear at an Assessment Appeals Board hearing to present and explain his appraisal. That time is billed separately and agreed before you order. Many owners present the report themselves, which the board permits. If a tax consultant or attorney represents you, Brian can coordinate with them on what the hearing will require.

Can I appeal if I just bought the house?

Often a purchase price is strong evidence of market value, so the Assessor usually enrolls it. But if you paid a premium for reasons that do not reflect market value, or the transfer was not an arm's-length sale, an appeal may be possible. Supplemental assessment appeals have short deadlines after the notice is mailed, so act quickly and check the date on your notice.
