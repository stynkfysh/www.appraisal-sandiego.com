Pre-Purchase Appraisal

An independent value before you commit to buying a San Diego County home.

Buying a home is usually the largest check you will ever write, and the price is often set under pressure. A lender's appraisal only happens if you finance, and it is ordered for the lender, not for you. Cash buyers, buyers in off-market or family deals, and anyone facing a property with few clear comparables can benefit from an independent number. For buyers in San Diego County who want a value before they sign, Brian Ward provides pre-purchase appraisals.

Who benefits from a pre-purchase appraisal

Cash buyers have no lender appraisal to lean on, so the price they agree to is the only check. Buyers purchasing from a relative, a landlord or a neighbor without the market testing the price want to confirm it is fair. Buyers of unusual properties, such as rural acreage near Valley Center, a hillside home in La Mesa with a steep driveway, or a condo in a complex with few recent sales, often find that online estimates swing widely. An appraisal grounded in actual comparables gives them a firmer footing.

Buyers relocating from outside the county face another challenge. Prices and housing types differ sharply between places like Chula Vista, Poway and Oceanside, and a home that looks like a bargain online may be priced right for a street with issues you cannot see from a listing. An independent appraisal gives you local context before you commit. It also helps you judge whether competing offers are pushing the price beyond what sales support.

How the timing works in escrow

Most San Diego County purchases use the standard California residential purchase agreement, which by default gives buyers 17 days for investigation and appraisal contingencies unless the parties agree otherwise. That is not much time. Brian schedules pre-purchase inspections quickly and can deliver on a rush basis when needed, so you have the report before your contingency deadline. If an inspection reveals problems, he can factor them into the value when you share the report.

If you are still negotiating, an appraisal before you write an offer helps you set a ceiling. If you are in escrow already, it can back up asking for a lower price or canceling while your contingency period is still open. Knowing the market value also helps if a lender's appraisal comes in low and you need to decide whether to bring more cash or renegotiate. Your agent and attorney can explain your rights under the specific contract you sign.

What Brian looks at for buyers

Brian approaches a pre-purchase appraisal as he would any other: the comparables decide the value. He looks at the things that affect resale in San Diego County, including fire hazard severity zones in the East County foothills, Mello-Roos and HOA costs in newer master-planned communities, private septic and well systems in rural areas, flood zones near rivers and creeks, and permit history for additions. He notes items that may matter to a lender if you plan to refinance later. The goal is a number you can weigh against the asking price with clear reasons behind it.

Picking the right report

A Standard appraisal with an interior inspection gives the most complete picture, and it is the usual choice if you have access during escrow. When the seller will not allow an extra visit, a Drive-By appraisal based on the exterior and listing photos can still be useful. For a quick read on whether an asking price is in the right range before you make an offer, a Desktop or Basic Desktop report costs less and comes back faster.

Questions about this appraisal

Can my lender use this appraisal for my loan?

No. Federal appraiser independence rules require lenders to order appraisals through their own process, so a borrower-ordered report cannot be used for the loan. A pre-purchase appraisal is for your own decision. If you finance, the lender will order a separate appraisal later in escrow. The two reports may not match, since each appraiser chooses comparables independently, but both should be supported by data.

Can you finish before my contingency deadline?

Often, yes, if you call early. Timing depends on the property and access, and rush service is available for an added fee. Tell Brian your deadline when you call, along with access details from the listing agent, and he will tell you honestly whether the timing works before you order.

What if the appraisal comes in below the price?

That gives you information to act on. You might ask the seller to lower the price, ask for credits, decide the home is still worth it to you, or cancel within your contingency period if your contract allows. Your agent and attorney can advise on the options under your specific contract. The appraisal shows the comparables, which helps if you negotiate.

Is this the same as a home inspection?

No. Condition is noted only as far as it changes value, but does not test systems, crawl through attics, or evaluate structure the way a licensed home inspector does. Many buyers order both. The inspection tells you what is wrong, and the appraisal tells you what the home is worth with those issues considered.
