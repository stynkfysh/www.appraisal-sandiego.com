Expert Witness Appraisal

Litigation appraisals and testimony for San Diego County real estate disputes.

When property value is at the center of a lawsuit, the appraisal has to survive cross-examination. Opposing counsel will look for weak comparables, unexplained adjustments and any sign of advocacy. Brian Ward prepares appraisals for litigation in San Diego County and can testify about his work in depositions, mediations, arbitrations and trials. With more than 20 years and 7,000 appraisals behind him, he focuses on reports that are clear to a judge or jury and consistent with appraisal standards.

Who hires an appraisal expert

Attorneys hire appraisal experts in many kinds of cases: partition actions between co-owners, divorce and estate disputes, disputes over a property's value at a past sale, construction defect and property damage cases, elder financial abuse claims involving a below-market transfer, and easement or boundary disputes that affect value. Sometimes the need is a new appraisal. Other times it is a review of the opposing side's appraisal to test whether its comparables and adjustments hold up. Brian works with attorneys on either side of a case.

Timing matters in litigation. Expert disclosure deadlines, discovery cutoffs and trial dates leave little room for delays, so contact Brian early, even before you are sure an appraisal will be needed. An initial conversation about the property and the issues can help your attorney decide whether a full appraisal, a review or a consulting role makes the most sense. Early involvement also leaves time to inspect the property before conditions change.

Appraisal standards and testimony

USPAP binds every appraiser, and it requires the opinion to be independent and free of bias. That matters in litigation, because an expert who simply argues the client's number loses credibility quickly. Brian's role is to give an honest value and explain clearly how he reached it. Courts and opposing experts are quick to spot a report that reads like an argument, and once credibility is lost, it is hard to win back.

Litigation work often involves retrospective dates, such as the date of a sale, a transfer, a death or a defect discovery. It may also involve hypothetical conditions, such as the value of a property without a defect. Brian can also prepare an appraisal review, which evaluates another appraiser's work for completeness, accuracy and reasonableness without necessarily offering a new value. Your attorney decides which approach serves the case, and each choice should be made early.

What Brian brings to San Diego County cases

Brian has appraised homes across San Diego County since 2004, which gives him working knowledge of how values differ between areas and over time. In a case about a 2015 sale in Encinitas or a defect claim on a newer home in San Marcos, that history helps him choose comparables a judge can understand. He prepares exhibits that show the comparable sales, adjustments and photos in plain terms, and he explains appraisal concepts without jargon. Deposition and trial preparation, appearance time and travel are billed separately and agreed in writing.

Choosing the right report

Most litigation calls for a Standard appraisal with an interior inspection when access is available, because a thorough report is easier to defend. When the property has changed or access is denied, a Drive-By or Desktop report based on records and exterior observation can be used, with its limits clearly stated. For small rental buildings, the 2-4 Unit reports apply. Discuss the case with Brian early, so the scope fits the legal issues.

Appraisal fees start at $699 for a Standard single-family report, with retrospective, review and testimony work quoted separately based on the case. Brian provides the scope and fee in writing before work begins, so the attorney and client can budget for the full engagement, not just the report. Changes in scope, such as an added effective date or a rebuttal report, are quoted in writing before the extra work begins.

Questions about this appraisal

How is testimony billed?

Testimony, deposition preparation, meetings with counsel, appearance time and travel are billed separately from the appraisal fee. The terms are set in writing before the work begins, so there are no surprises. Brian will discuss the expected scope with your attorney and provide a quote for the appraisal and an hourly or day rate for testimony-related time.

Will you critique a report someone else wrote?

Yes. A review checks whether a different appraiser's work is complete, accurate and reasonable. It can identify problems such as poor comparable selection, unsupported adjustments or errors in the property description. A review may or may not include a new value opinion, depending on what your attorney needs for the case.

Will you give the value my client needs?

No, and that protects your client. Appraisers must provide independent, unbiased opinions under professional standards. A report that argues for a predetermined number is easy to attack on cross-examination. What Brian can do is make sure the facts that support your position, such as condition issues or location factors, are accurately documented and fairly considered.

Can you value a property for an earlier date?

Yes. Looking back in time is ordinary work, and these reports are common in litigation, using sales and records from around the date in question. Brian can value a property as of a sale date, transfer date, date of death or the date a defect was discovered. The older the effective date, the more the analysis depends on records, so give us all the documentation you have.

Usually the right fit

A Standard appraisal ($699, or $799 for 2-4 units) usually fits litigation, since a full inspection is easier to defend under cross-examination. A Drive-By ($649) or Desktop ($449) applies when access is limited. Retrospective dates, reviews and testimony are quoted separately based on the case.
