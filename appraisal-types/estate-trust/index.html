Estate and Trust Appraisal

Clear, supportable values for trustees, executors and beneficiaries settling a San Diego estate.

Settling a trust or estate means making decisions other people will review. Beneficiaries want to know the house was valued fairly. A sibling buying out the others wants a number nobody can call a favor. Your attorney and CPA need something on paper for the accounting and the tax returns. Brian Ward prepares estate and trust appraisals for San Diego County homes, condos and small multi-unit properties, with the effective date, scope and intended users stated plainly so the report fits the job you actually have.

Who orders an estate or trust appraisal

Successor trustees are the most common clients. When a living trust becomes irrevocable, the trustee takes on a duty to manage the assets, keep beneficiaries reasonably informed and account for what happens to the property. A current appraisal supports nearly every decision that follows: whether to sell, what to list for, how to equalize shares when one child keeps the house and another takes cash, and what to report to the CPA. Executors in probate, beneficiaries checking a proposed buyout and attorneys preparing for mediation order the same work.

Probate, trusts and the effective date

In a California probate, real estate on the inventory is usually valued by a probate referee appointed by the court, unless the court allows otherwise. Many families still order a private appraisal alongside it, to set a list price, to check the referee's figure, or to support basis. Trust administration is different. There is no referee, so the trustee is responsible for getting a credible value. Either way, the trustee should keep the report with the trust records.

The effective date depends on the purpose. Basis and inventory usually call for the date of death. A buyout or in-kind distribution usually calls for a current date, close to when the deal is signed. Some trusts need both, which Brian can deliver in one engagement with two clearly labeled values. Your attorney can tell you which dates the trust terms and California Probate Code require in your case. Ordering both at once also avoids a second inspection later.

How Brian works estate files in San Diego County

Estate homes often have been lived in for decades, and the condition shows it: original kitchens in Allied Gardens, deferred roofs in Spring Valley, add-ons of uncertain permit history in Lemon Grove. Brian measures the house, photographs each room, checks the County's recorded square footage against what he finds and notes items a buyer would price in. That honest condition picture matters because a beneficiary who later sees the home sell for more than the appraisal will want to know why. A clear report usually answers that question before it turns into a dispute.

Picking the right report

For a buyout, a sale decision or anything likely to be scrutinized, the Standard appraisal with a full interior inspection is the safer choice. For a straightforward inventory where the home is in typical condition and nobody is contesting value, a Desktop report can be enough and costs less. Duplexes, triplexes and fourplexes, common in older neighborhoods like City Heights and Normal Heights, use the 2-4 Unit versions, which add rental analysis to the sales comparison.

A Basic Desktop begins at $299 and a Standard single-family report at $699, and your quote is locked in before scheduling. If the trust holds several properties, such as a house in Escondido and a condo in Pacific Beach, Brian can quote them together and coordinate inspections so the trustee is not juggling separate appointments and timelines. Ask for the scope in writing if your attorney needs particular items covered.

Questions about this appraisal

Can one report give both a date-of-death and a current value?

Yes. Brian can prepare two values in one engagement, each with its own effective date and supporting comparables. This is common when a trustee needs the date-of-death figure for basis and a current figure for a buyout or distribution. The second value adds some work, so the quote reflects it, but it costs less than two separate assignments and keeps the analysis consistent.

Do I still need an appraisal if the court appoints a probate referee?

Not always. The referee's appraisal satisfies the court inventory. Families order a private appraisal for other reasons: setting a list price, checking a value that looks off, supporting a buyout, or documenting basis with an interior inspection the referee may not have done. Ask your probate attorney whether a separate report would help in your case.

One sibling lives in the house. Does that cause problems?

It can, but it is common. Brian schedules the inspection with whoever has access and treats every party the same. He does not advocate for the occupant or the other beneficiaries. The report describes the property as it is and states who the intended users are. If the relationship is tense, let him know so he can coordinate through the trustee or the attorney instead.

How long does an estate appraisal take?

Timing depends on the property, the effective date and the season. Retrospective dates, multiple values, large lots and unusual homes take more time than a typical house with a current date. If your deadline is short, rush delivery can be added for an extra fee. You will get a delivery date along with the fixed quote before anything is scheduled.

Usually the right fit

A Desktop ($449) or Basic Desktop ($299) can cover an uncontested inventory or a quick planning number when the house is typical for its area.
