Insurance Claim Appraisal

Before-and-after market values for property losses, disputes and diminished value claims.

After a fire, flood, landslide or construction mistake, the question is often bigger than the repair bill. What was the home worth before the damage, what is it worth now, and does the history of the loss still hold the value down after repairs? Brian Ward prepares market value appraisals for insurance and property damage disputes in San Diego County. He is a real estate appraiser, not an adjuster or contractor, so his role is to measure what the market says about value, with the effective dates your attorney or adjuster needs.

Who needs an insurance claim appraisal

Homeowners in a dispute with their insurer over a total loss often need a credible pre-loss market value. Owners whose homes were damaged by a neighbor's retaining wall failure, a contractor's error, a utility line break or a slope failure may have a claim against that third party for the drop in value. Public adjusters, attorneys and insurers themselves order appraisals to settle the amount in dispute. San Diego County has seen major wildfires, such as the Cedar Fire in 2003 and the Witch Fire in 2007, and serious flooding in January 2023, so these questions come up across the county.

The appraisal clause and diminished value

Most California homeowners policies include an appraisal clause, drawn from the standard fire policy in the Insurance Code. When the insured and the insurer disagree on the amount of a loss, either side can demand appraisal. Each selects a competent, disinterested appraiser, the two choose an umpire, and an agreement by any two sets the amount of loss. That process addresses the amount of loss, not whether the policy covers it.

Diminished value is the loss in market value that remains after repairs, because buyers discount a home with a known history of fire, flooding, slope movement or mold. Many homeowners policies do not pay for diminished value, so these claims are more often made against a responsible third party. Whether you have a claim, and against whom, is a legal question for your attorney. The appraisal measures how much value was lost.

How Brian approaches damage cases in San Diego County

For a before-and-after analysis, Brian values the home as of a date just before the loss, using sales that closed around then, and values it again in its damaged or repaired state. For diminished value, he looks for market evidence of how buyers treat comparable homes with similar histories, such as repaired fire damage in the East County foothills, flood-affected homes in lower-lying neighborhoods, or houses near known slide areas. He explains the method and the data, so the opposing side can follow the reasoning even if they disagree.

Timing affects the evidence. Photos taken right after a loss, contractor estimates and adjuster notes are much easier to gather early than months later, when repairs may have covered up the damage. If you think you may need a value analysis, contact Brian before the repairs are finished, so he can see and photograph the property in its damaged state. That record can be hard to rebuild later, and it often decides how strong the analysis is.

Choosing the report

Most claim and litigation work calls for a Standard appraisal with an interior inspection, because condition before and after the loss is the heart of the analysis. If the home has been demolished or rebuilt, Brian may work from photos, permits and records, in which case a Desktop report can fit. Assignments with two effective dates, retrospective dates, or testimony cost more than a single current appraisal, and the quote reflects the actual scope.

Questions about this appraisal

Can you serve as the appraiser under my policy's appraisal clause?

Brian can be considered for a role in the appraisal process when the dispute involves the market value of real estate. The policy and the Insurance Code require each appraiser to be competent and disinterested, so he will discuss the specifics, including any prior relationship with the parties, before accepting. For disputes about repair or construction costs, a contractor or estimator may be a better fit.

Is diminished value covered by my homeowners policy?

Often it is not, but policies vary. Many homeowners policies pay to repair or replace damaged property and do not pay for the lingering loss in market value. Diminished value claims are more often brought against a third party who caused the damage. Ask your attorney or public adjuster to review your policy and the facts of your loss.

Do you estimate repair or rebuilding costs?

No. What Brian appraises is market value: the price the property would bring from a typical buyer. Replacement cost and repair estimates come from contractors, estimators or adjusters. The two figures answer different questions, and in many claims both are needed. Brian's report can use repair estimates as an input when valuing a damaged home.

How do you value a home that no longer exists?

Brian builds the pre-loss value from records: permits, prior listing photos, the County's property data, your own photos and descriptions, and comparable sales from before the loss. The report states what information was relied on and what assumptions were made. The more documentation you can provide about the home's features and condition, the stronger the result.

Usually the right fit

A Standard appraisal ($699, or $799 for 2-4 units) usually fits, because condition before and after the loss drives the analysis. A Desktop ($449) can work when the home was demolished or rebuilt and records are the main evidence. Retrospective dates, before-and-after values and testimony add to the fee.
