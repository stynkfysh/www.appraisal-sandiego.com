Family Transfer Appraisal

Documented values for gifts, sales and Prop 19 transfers between family members.

Moving a home between family members is rarely just a deed. A gift can trigger a federal gift tax return. A sale to a child below market price is part gift. An inheritance or transfer may change the property tax bill under Proposition 19. Each of these questions turns on market value at a specific date. Brian Ward prepares family transfer appraisals for San Diego County homes, giving parents, children, attorneys and CPAs a documented number to plan around instead of a figure pulled from a website.

Who orders a family transfer appraisal

When parents give a child a home, or part of one, the gift tax return often requires an appraisal. Families selling a house to a relative want a price that is fair to the other heirs and defensible if anyone asks later. Adult children inheriting a parent's home want to know how Prop 19 will affect the property tax before they decide to move in, rent it out or sell. Estate planning attorneys and CPAs order these reports to support the paperwork they prepare.

How Prop 19 changed parent-child transfers

Before 2021, parents could pass a home and other real estate to their children without property tax reassessment in many cases. Proposition 19 narrowed that. Now the parent-child exclusion generally covers only the family home, and only if the child makes it their principal residence within one year and files for the homeowners' exemption. Even then, the exclusion is limited to the home's factored base year value plus a set amount, originally $1 million and adjusted for inflation every two years. Market value above that limit is added to the new assessment. That is why families often order an appraisal before deciding who should move in.

So market value at the transfer date directly affects the property tax bill. Rentals and second homes passing to children are generally reassessed in full. Grandparent-grandchild transfers can qualify only in narrow cases. Claims must be filed with the San Diego County Assessor within set deadlines. Your attorney or CPA should confirm how the current rules and amounts apply before you transfer. The Assessor also offers forms and guidance on its website.

Gifts, sales and federal tax basics

A gift of real estate above the annual exclusion amount usually requires a federal gift tax return, even if no tax is due. If you want the IRS statute of limitations to run on that gift, the return generally needs adequate disclosure, which often means a qualified appraisal. Gifted property also carries over the giver's tax basis rather than stepping up, which can mean a bigger capital gain for the child down the road. That tradeoff is worth discussing with your CPA before choosing a gift over an inheritance.

Brian values the home at the transfer date, documents condition and explains the comparables, so the report can be attached to a return or kept in the family file. If several children share ownership, or one child buys out another, the same report can support those calculations. Keeping a copy with your estate papers also helps whoever settles things later, since it records what the home was like and what it was worth on the day it changed hands.

Matching the report to the transfer

For a gift tax return or a family sale that other heirs may question, the Standard appraisal with an interior inspection is the stronger choice. For early planning, such as estimating whether a Prop 19 transfer would trigger partial reassessment on a long-held Point Loma or La Mesa home, a Desktop or Basic Desktop report gives a credible estimate at lower cost. Small rental buildings use the 2-4 Unit versions.

Questions about this appraisal

Will the Assessor use my appraisal under Prop 19?

The Assessor determines the value it enrolls. Your appraisal is evidence. It helps you estimate ahead of time whether a transfer will trigger partial reassessment, and it gives you something concrete to discuss if the Assessor's value seems high. It is not binding on the county, but a well-supported report is a solid basis for a review request.

Is a gift or an inheritance better for taxes?

It depends on the family. Inherited property usually gets a stepped-up basis, while gifted property carries over the parent's original basis, which can mean more capital gain later. Property tax treatment under Prop 19 can also differ depending on timing and use. These are planning questions for your CPA and estate attorney; the appraisal gives them the value to work with.

Can we sell the house to our son for less than market value?

That's allowed, but for federal tax purposes the gap between the sale price and market value is usually considered a gift. An appraisal documents what market value was, so your CPA can report the gift portion correctly. It also helps show other family members that the pricing decision was made with a clear view of the numbers.

How soon after the transfer should the appraisal be done?

As close to the transfer date as practical, or before it if you are still planning. If the transfer already happened, Brian can prepare a retrospective appraisal as of that date. Filing deadlines for Prop 19 claims and gift tax returns are not flexible, so check them with your advisor and leave enough time for the report.

Usually the right fit

A Standard appraisal ($699, or $799 for 2-4 units) usually fits for gift tax returns and family sales, since the inspection supports a value that may be reviewed by the IRS or other heirs. A Desktop ($449) or Basic Desktop ($299) works well for early Prop 19 planning estimates before the transfer is decided.
