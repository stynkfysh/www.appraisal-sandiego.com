Immigration I-864 Appraisal

Documented home equity for an I-864 Affidavit of Support asset calculation.

When a sponsor's income falls short of the Form I-864 requirement, assets can help close the gap, and real estate is often the largest asset a sponsor owns. USCIS and the State Department will want to see how the net value was calculated, and that means a credible market value from a licensed appraiser. Brian Ward prepares appraisals of San Diego County homes for I-864 asset documentation, for sponsors, joint sponsors and the immigration attorneys who prepare their filings.

Who needs an I-864 appraisal

Petitioning sponsors whose income is below the required level are the most common clients. Joint sponsors, who agree to take on the same financial obligation, use appraisals when their own income is not enough on its own. Household members who sign Form I-864A may also have real estate that counts. Immigration attorneys often recommend the appraisal when a sponsor owns a home with significant equity but has a modest or irregular income, such as a retiree or a self-employed owner.

How assets count on the I-864

The sponsor generally must show household income of at least 125 percent of the federal poverty guidelines for the household size, or 100 percent for certain active-duty military sponsors. If income falls short, assets can make up the difference. The net value of assets usually must equal five times the shortfall, or three times when the sponsor is a U.S. citizen petitioning for a spouse or an adult child, with a lower multiple for certain adopted children. An attorney can run those numbers before you order.

For real estate, net value means market value minus all mortgages and liens. According to the form instructions, you need proof that you own the property, a current appraisal from a licensed appraiser, and documentation of loans and liens. Assets generally must be convertible to cash within one year without significant hardship. Rules change, so have your immigration attorney confirm the current requirements and multiples for your case. Keep copies of every document submitted with the affidavit.

Questions about this appraisal

Can I use my primary residence as an asset?

Under the current form instructions, you may count real estate such as a home at its net value, as long as you document that you own it, include a recent appraisal from a licensed appraiser and show the liens against it. Policy can change, and officers can question whether an asset is readily available. Your immigration attorney should confirm how your home will be treated in your case.

Is an online estimate good enough?

Generally not. The instructions call for an appraisal by a licensed appraiser, and an automated estimate does not meet that standard. A signed appraisal also shows the officer the comparables and reasoning behind the number, which helps if the value is questioned. Submitting a weak valuation can lead to requests for more evidence and delays.

How current must the appraisal be?

The instructions ask for a recent appraisal, without setting a precise age limit. As a practical matter, a report from within the past several months is safer, especially if values have changed. If your filing or interview is delayed, ask your attorney whether an updated value is needed. Brian can update a report he prepared.

Can a joint sponsor use their home equity?

Yes. A joint sponsor can count assets, including real estate, under the same rules as the main sponsor. The joint sponsor must meet the requirement on their own, so their income and assets cannot be combined with the main sponsor's. Brian can appraise the joint sponsor's property separately, and the report will name them as the client.
