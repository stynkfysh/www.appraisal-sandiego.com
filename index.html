Four steps, no surprises

  1. 1

    Send the address and the reason

    Call, email or use the form. Give us the property address, what the appraisal is for and the date the value should apply to. If an attorney or CPA is involved, tell us. That one detail decides which report type fits and what the report has to say.

  2. 2

    Get a fixed quote and a date

    You get a written fee before anything is scheduled. It does not move later because the house turned out bigger or the comps were harder to find. We also give you a delivery date, so you can plan around a court deadline, a filing window or a family meeting.

  3. 3

    Inspection or records study

    For a standard report, Brian walks the interior and exterior, measures the home and photographs it. A typical visit is fairly short, often around an hour. For a desktop report, he works from public records, MLS history, permits and photos you provide. Either way, the comparable sales research happens afterward at his desk.

  4. 4

    Signed report delivered

    You receive a signed PDF that states the value, the effective date, the intended use and the reasoning behind the number. It names the comparable sales and explains the adjustments in plain terms. If your attorney or CPA has a question about it, Brian answers it directly.

Why a local certified appraiser, not a guess

The number has to hold up

An online estimate or an agent's price opinion is fine for curiosity. It is not built to survive an opposing attorney, an IRS review or a county assessor. A certified appraisal follows national standards, shows its comparable sales and explains every adjustment, so the people reading it can check the math.

Local sales, not ZIP code averages

Two streets in the same ZIP can sell tens of thousands of dollars apart because of a canyon view, a freeway, a flight path or a newer HOA. Brian has spent over twenty years watching how San Diego County homes actually sell, block by block, and picks comparables with that in mind.

Past dates done correctly

Estates, trusts and divorces often need a value as of a date months or years ago. That means using sales from around that date, not today's market, and describing the home as it was then. Retrospective work is routine here, and the report says clearly which date the value applies to.

Written for your purpose

A lender appraisal is written for a bank. Yours is written for you and the people you name, such as your attorney, CPA, trustee or the court. The intended use is stated in the report, which matters when someone later asks why the appraisal was done and who can rely on it.

No stake in the outcome

Brian is paid the same fee no matter what value the research supports. He is not selling the house, buying it or representing either side of a dispute. That independence is the point of the report, and it is the first thing an opposing party or a judge will look for.

Quick answers

How much does a private appraisal cost?

Pricing begins at $299 for the Basic Desktop; the Desktop is $449, the Drive-By $649, and the Standard single-family report with a full inspection $699, while 2-4 unit properties run $549 for a Desktop and $799 for a Standard. Rush jobs, large or unusual properties, retrospective dates and court testimony can cost more. You get a fixed quote before anything is scheduled, and it does not change afterward.

How long does it take?

How fast the report is done depends on its type, the property and the current workload. Desktop reports usually move faster than reports with an inspection. Retrospective dates, acreage and unusual homes can take longer because the comparable research is harder. You get a delivery date with your quote. If you have a court date or filing deadline, tell us up front and we will say whether rush service can meet it.

Can you appraise a house for an earlier date?

Yes. This is called a retrospective appraisal, and it is common for date-of-death values, trust administration, divorce and tax matters. The value is based on sales from around the effective date, not today's market, and the home is described as it was at that time, as best the records and your information allow. The report states the effective date clearly.

What is the difference between a desktop and an inspection appraisal?

A desktop appraisal is done without visiting the home. Brian uses public records, MLS history, permits and any photos or details you provide. With a Standard appraisal, I inspect the inside and outside of the home in full, measure it and take photos. A Drive-By looks at the exterior only. Desktops cost less and move faster. An inspection is better when condition, upgrades or additions are in question.

Who can order an appraisal?

Anyone with a legitimate reason: homeowners, buyers, sellers, heirs, trustees, executors, attorneys, CPAs and financial advisers. The person who orders and pays is the client, and you can name others as intended users, such as your attorney or the other party in a divorce. If the house is occupied by someone else, we will need a way to arrange access for an inspection.
