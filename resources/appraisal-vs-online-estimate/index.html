Appraisal vs. Online Estimate

Why automated online home values often miss in San Diego County, what a certified appraisal does differently, and when each one is good enough for your purpose.

How online estimates work

Automated valuation models, the engines behind online home values, use public records, recent nearby sales and statistical formulas. They know the lot size, the recorded square footage, the bedroom count and what similar homes nearby sold for. They can produce a number in seconds for almost any address, which is why they are so popular. What they cannot do is see the house. They do not know whether the kitchen was remodeled last year or still has 1978 cabinets, whether the backyard looks at the ocean or a freeway wall, or whether a garage conversion was permitted. They rely on records that are often out of date. Their own published error ranges can be wide, especially for unusual homes.

Where they miss in San Diego County

This county is hard on automated models. Coastal neighborhoods have value swings over a single block because of views and distance to the beach. Older central neighborhoods have homes that were enlarged without the records being updated. Rural areas have acreage, wells, groves and outbuildings that models barely understand. Master-planned suburbs have Mello-Roos taxes and HOA dues that change what buyers will pay. Canyon lots, flight paths, busy streets and fire zones also affect value in ways a model may not catch. The result is that two online sites can give the same house very different numbers. Neither is necessarily wrong by its own logic, but neither has looked at the property.

What a certified appraisal adds

A certified appraiser looks at the actual home, or at its records and photos in a desktop report, and picks comparable sales that match on the features that matter. The appraiser then adjusts for differences such as size, condition, view and lot, and explains each adjustment. The work follows national appraisal standards and is signed by a state-certified professional. Just as important, the report states its purpose, its effective date and who may rely on it. That is what makes it usable for an estate, a divorce, a tax appeal or a court case. An online estimate carries no such responsibility. Nobody signs it, and nobody can be asked to explain it.

When an online estimate is fine

For curiosity, a rough sense of equity or deciding whether to talk to an agent, an online estimate is a reasonable starting point. Look at several sites and treat the range, not any single number, as the useful part. Compare it with recent sales of homes very similar to yours on your street. Where it falls short is any decision with money, taxes or another party involved. Splitting an estate between siblings, buying out a spouse, setting a tax basis, appealing a property tax assessment or presenting evidence to a court all call for a value someone has to stand behind. That is the job of an appraisal.

Choosing the right level of appraisal

Not every situation needs a full inspection. A Basic Desktop appraisal at $299 or a Desktop at $449 gives a certified value without a visit and is often enough for planning or for an amicable estate. A Standard appraisal with an interior and exterior inspection, starting at $699, is better when condition matters or the value may be challenged. If you are unsure, start by describing your situation. Brian will suggest the report that fits and explain why. The goal is a value that holds up for your purpose without paying for more report than you need.
