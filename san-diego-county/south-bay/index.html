South Bay

Private appraisals in 10 South Bay communities.

South Bay runs from National City and Chula Vista down through Otay Mesa and San Ysidro to the border, with Imperial Beach on the ocean and Coronado across the bay. It mixes older neighborhoods west of Interstate 805 with large planned communities to the east, plus a resort-style island town with its own market entirely. A home's value here can hinge on whether it is east or west of the freeway, which side of the bay or border it is on, and how near it sits to the water.

We see a lot of family transfer, estate and immigration-related appraisal work here, along with divorce cases. Many homes have been in one family for decades, and many have additions or converted garages that need to be described accurately. Converted garages and room additions are common in the older neighborhoods. We compare what is in the records with what is actually there and explain the difference. Coronado and the newer planned areas of eastern Chula Vista call for a different set of comparables entirely, and the report explains which ones were used.

What makes appraising here different

Western Chula Vista and National City have mostly older single-family homes, many expanded over the years, and permit history matters. Eastlake, Otay Ranch and the rest of eastern Chula Vista went up largely from the 1990s onward, and their HOAs and Mello-Roos taxes influence what buyers are willing to pay. Imperial Beach is a small beach city with modest older homes and condos, where nearness to the ocean drives value and the sewage-related water quality issues have weighed on the area. Coronado has very few sales of truly comparable homes, and the Village and the Cays behave differently. San Ysidro and Otay Mesa sit near the border and the freeways, which affects both demand and noise.
