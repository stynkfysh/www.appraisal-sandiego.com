Central San Diego

Private appraisals in 28 Central San Diego communities.

Central San Diego covers Downtown, the Uptown neighborhoods such as Hillcrest and Mission Hills, North Park, the Mid-City communities, the Navajo area, Mission Valley and Southeastern San Diego. Some of the county's oldest homes are here, such as early-1900s Craftsman bungalows and Spanish Revival houses, mixed in with high-rise condos and newer townhomes. Many of these neighborhoods are walkable and close to Balboa Park, and buyers pay attention to which block a home is on.

Our work here leans toward estates, divorces and small multi-unit properties. Many lots have a second unit, a granny flat or a newer ADU, and the appraisal has to sort out what is permitted, what earns rent and what a buyer would actually pay for it. Downtown and Mission Valley condos also come up in estate work, where HOA documents, parking and the building's history all need a look. Older homes may have decades of changes that never made it into the county records, so we compare what is there with what is recorded.

What makes appraising here different

Housing changes block by block. Kensington, North Park and Normal Heights offer older homes that keep original details, and some are in historic districts or under Mills Act contracts that reduce property taxes. Downtown and Mission Valley are mostly condos, where HOA dues, building age, parking and floor level drive value. Navajo neighborhoods such as San Carlos, Allied Gardens and Del Cerro are mostly mid-century single-family homes. Southeastern San Diego has a wide mix of older homes and lot sizes. ADUs are common across the area, and permits often lag behind what was built. Parts of Mission Valley also sit in the San Diego River floodplain.
