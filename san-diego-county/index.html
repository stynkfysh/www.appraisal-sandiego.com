San Diego County, community by community

121 cities, neighborhoods and rural communities from the coast to the desert.

San Diego County has 18 incorporated cities, from Oceanside in the north to Imperial Beach in the south and El Cajon in the east, plus dozens of unincorporated communities that the county itself governs. Those include places like Fallbrook, Valley Center, Ramona, Spring Valley, Alpine, Julian and Borrego Springs. Land use, permits and some fees differ depending on which side of a city line a house sits. The incorporated cities include Carlsbad, Chula Vista, Escondido, La Mesa, Poway, San Marcos, Santee and Vista, along with the City of San Diego itself.

The housing is just as varied. Within one county you have beach lots, high-rise condos, post-war tracts, master-planned suburbs, horse properties, avocado groves, mountain cabins and desert homes. Each needs its own set of comparable sales and its own review of wells, septic, wildfire risk, HOAs or Mello-Roos. Choose your area below to see how we approach appraisals there. Prices and buyer demand can shift from one community to the next, so the comparables need to come from where buyers actually shop. We cover every part of the county.

North County Coast

This stretch runs from Oceanside south through Carlsbad, Encinitas and Solana Beach to Del Mar, with Rancho Santa Fe set a few miles inland. It is one of the most varied pieces of coastline in the state. You can find 1950s beach cottages, bluff-top homes, newer tract neighborhoods east of Interstate 5 and multi-acre estates, sometimes within a ten-minute drive of each other. Value along this coast can change more in one block than it does across some entire inland ZIP codes.

Inland North County

Inland North County covers Vista, San Marcos and Escondido along State Route 78, then reaches north and east into Valley Center, Fallbrook and Pala. The cities have dense subdivisions, condos and older neighborhoods near their downtowns. Past the city edges, the land opens into avocado and citrus groves, horse properties and homes on several acres down private roads. The same drive can take you from a downtown Escondido bungalow to a grove-covered hillside in a few minutes.

I-15 Corridor

Along Interstate 15 north of Miramar sit Mira Mesa, Scripps Ranch, Rancho Penasquitos, Rancho Bernardo, 4S Ranch and the City of Poway. Most of this area was built out between the 1970s and the 2000s, in planned communities with schools, parks and shopping laid out together. It is largely single-family homes, with condos and townhomes mixed in near the main roads. Commutes, school boundaries and freeway access shape demand here, and buyers notice small differences between neighborhoods.

San Diego Coast & Mesas

This region covers the city's beach neighborhoods plus the communities a short way inland: Carmel Valley, University City, Clairemont, Point Loma, Ocean Beach, Mission Beach, Pacific Beach and La Jolla. In a short drive you go from narrow beach lots and walk streets to post-war hillside tracts, 1960s and 1970s ranch homes and newer master-planned neighborhoods. Much of this area lies inside the coastal zone, and some of it sits close to Interstate 5 or the flight paths around the region, which buyers weigh.

Central San Diego

Central San Diego covers Downtown, the Uptown neighborhoods such as Hillcrest and Mission Hills, North Park, the Mid-City communities, the Navajo area, Mission Valley and Southeastern San Diego. Some of the county's oldest homes are here, such as early-1900s Craftsman bungalows and Spanish Revival houses, mixed in with high-rise condos and newer townhomes. Many of these neighborhoods are walkable and close to Balboa Park, and buyers pay attention to which block a home is on.

South Bay

South Bay runs from National City and Chula Vista down through Otay Mesa and San Ysidro to the border, with Imperial Beach on the ocean and Coronado across the bay. It mixes older neighborhoods west of Interstate 805 with large planned communities to the east, plus a resort-style island town with its own market entirely. A home's value here can hinge on whether it is east or west of the freeway, which side of the bay or border it is on, and how near it sits to the water.

East County

East County covers El Cajon, La Mesa, Santee and Lakeside, plus unincorporated communities such as Spring Valley, Alpine and Jamul. Close in, you find post-war tracts and 1960s and 1970s neighborhoods. Farther east, lots grow larger, the hills get steeper and you see horse properties, ranchettes and custom homes on acreage. Many neighborhoods have views toward the mountains or across the valleys, and that can carry real weight in value.

Backcountry & Desert

The backcountry covers the eastern half of the county: Ramona, Julian, Pine Valley, Campo, Jacumba and Borrego Springs in the Anza-Borrego Desert. It ranges from Ramona's ranches and vineyards to Julian's mountain cabins and apple country, and down to desert homes surrounded by state park land. Distances are long and roads are winding, so we plan the visit around the drive and ask about access ahead of time. Sales are fewer, and each one has to be read carefully.
