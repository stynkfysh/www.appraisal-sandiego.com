Inland North County

Private appraisals in 14 Inland North County communities.

Inland North County covers Vista, San Marcos and Escondido along State Route 78, then reaches north and east into Valley Center, Fallbrook and Pala. The cities have dense subdivisions, condos and older neighborhoods near their downtowns. Past the city edges, the land opens into avocado and citrus groves, horse properties and homes on several acres down private roads. The same drive can take you from a downtown Escondido bungalow to a grove-covered hillside in a few minutes.

That range is why a single approach does not work here. A San Marcos townhome has dozens of recent sales to compare. A Valley Center home on five acres with a well, a barn and a grove may have only a handful in a year. We adjust the research to the property, and the report explains how the comparables were chosen. For rural homes, we ask about water, septic and outbuildings before the visit, so nothing important gets missed or guessed at.

What makes appraising here different

In the cities, housing ranges from 1960s and 1970s tract homes to newer master-planned neighborhoods such as San Elijo Hills, where HOA dues and Mello-Roos taxes affect what buyers will pay. In Fallbrook, Valley Center and Pala, many homes rely on wells and septic systems, sit in high fire hazard areas and have outbuildings, groves or unpermitted additions. Each of those changes value, and buyers react to them differently. Fire insurance cost and availability has become a real factor in rural sales. Usable acreage, road access and water source matter as much as square footage, so the comparables have to match on those points first.
