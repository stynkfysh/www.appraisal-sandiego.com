About Brian Ward

California Certified Residential Real Estate Appraiser since 2004.

Brian Ward is licensed by California as a Certified Residential Real Estate Appraiser. Since starting as a home appraiser in 2004, he has finished over 7,000 appraisals. That works out to two decades of watching San Diego County homes sell through rising markets, falling markets and everything between. Every report on this site is researched and signed by him personally. He has seen how values respond to fires, interest rates, new freeways and changing tastes, and that background shapes how he reads the local sales.

His practice focuses on private appraisal work, meaning appraisals not ordered by a lender. His clients include attorneys, CPAs, trustees, executors, homeowners, buyers and sellers. They need values for estates and trusts, divorce, bankruptcy, property tax appeals, family transfers, insurance claims and other legal, tax or family matters. Each report is written for that specific purpose and names the people who can rely on it. He also handles pre-listing and pre-purchase values for owners and buyers who want an independent number.

Appraisal San Diego covers San Diego County for Brian Ward Appraisal; the company's primary website is brianward.com. Brian serves every part of the county from his San Diego office on Paseo Del Sur (15877), Oceanside to San Ysidro and the beaches out to Borrego Springs. You can reach him at (619) 717-6588 or contact@appraisal-sandiego.com. Every assignment starts with a fixed quote and a delivery date. How he works

Show the work

Every report names the comparable sales, explains the adjustments and gives the reasons behind the final number. If an attorney, CPA, judge or assessor wants to check the value, they should be able to follow it from start to finish without guessing.

Fixed fees, stated up front

You get the fee in writing before anything is scheduled. It does not grow after the inspection or because the research took longer than expected. If a job is complex, that is reflected in the quote, not added later.

Independent every time

Brian is paid the same whatever value the research supports. He does not take sides in a dispute, list homes for sale or tie his fee to the result. An appraisal is only useful if the people reading it can trust that nobody steered the number.

Answer the actual question

A date-of-death value, a divorce buyout and a pre-listing check are different questions. Brian asks what the report is for, what date it applies to and who will read it, then writes the report around that purpose rather than using a one-size form.

Have a date and a reason? That is enough to start.
