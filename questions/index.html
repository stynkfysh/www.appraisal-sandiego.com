Questions people ask

Fees, timing, retrospective dates, inspections and how the report gets used.

How much does a private appraisal cost?

Pricing begins at $299 for the Basic Desktop; the Desktop is $449, the Drive-By $649, and the Standard single-family report with a full inspection $699, while 2-4 unit properties run $549 for a Desktop and $799 for a Standard. Rush jobs, large or unusual properties, retrospective dates and court testimony can cost more. You get a fixed quote before anything is scheduled, and it does not change afterward.

How long does it take?

How fast the report is done depends on its type, the property and the current workload. Desktop reports usually move faster than reports with an inspection. Retrospective dates, acreage and unusual homes can take longer because the comparable research is harder. You get a delivery date with your quote. If you have a court date or filing deadline, tell us up front and we will say whether rush service can meet it.

Can you appraise a house for an earlier date?

Yes. This is called a retrospective appraisal, and it is common for date-of-death values, trust administration, divorce and tax matters. The value is based on sales from around the effective date, not today's market, and the home is described as it was at that time, as best the records and your information allow. The report states the effective date clearly.

What is the difference between a desktop and an inspection appraisal?

A desktop appraisal is done without visiting the home. Brian uses public records, MLS history, permits and any photos or details you provide. With a Standard appraisal, I inspect the inside and outside of the home in full, measure it and take photos. A Drive-By looks at the exterior only. Desktops cost less and move faster. An inspection is better when condition, upgrades or additions are in question.

Who can order an appraisal?

Anyone with a legitimate reason: homeowners, buyers, sellers, heirs, trustees, executors, attorneys, CPAs and financial advisers. The person who orders and pays is the client, and you can name others as intended users, such as your attorney or the other party in a divorce. If the house is occupied by someone else, we will need a way to arrange access for an inspection.

Will a court accept your appraisal?

Brian's reports are prepared by a California Certified Residential Real Estate Appraiser under the national appraisal standards, which is what courts, attorneys and the IRS expect to see. Whether a specific court or agency accepts a report is up to them, but a properly certified, well-supported appraisal is the standard evidence. Expert witness testimony is available when a case calls for it, at an added fee.

What sets this apart from an appraisal ordered by a lender?

A bank orders a lender appraisal, it is prepared for that bank, and it generally is not handed out to other parties. A private appraisal is ordered by you, for your purpose, and you choose who can rely on it. The research standards are similar, but the report addresses your question, such as a date-of-death value or a fair buyout figure, rather than loan approval.

Why not just use a Zestimate or online estimate?

Online estimates come from automated models that use public records and recent sales. They do not see the inside of the house, the condition, the remodel or the busy street behind the back fence. They are not signed by anyone and cannot be defended in a dispute or tax filing. An appraisal is a licensed professional's opinion, with the comparables and reasoning shown.

What happens during the inspection?

Brian walks through each room, notes condition, updates and features, measures the outside of the home and takes photos inside and out. He also looks at the lot, garage and any outbuildings or additions. A typical visit is fairly short, often around an hour. He does not test systems or open walls like a home inspector. It helps to have permits or a list of upgrades ready.

How and when do I pay?

You pay once the report has been delivered. You can pay by card, check or bank transfer. Law firms and other professional offices can arrange invoicing. The quoted fee is the fee you pay. It does not change because the house turned out larger than the records said. If you need a receipt for an estate or trust account, one is provided.

Can I be there during the inspection?

Yes, and it often helps. You can point out improvements, repairs or problems that are not obvious, and answer questions about the home's history. If you cannot be there, a family member, agent, tenant or attorney's representative can provide access. In divorce or disputed matters, it is best to tell us in advance who will attend.

Can you appraise a house with tenants in it?

Yes. We coordinate with you on access and on the notice the tenant needs under California law. The tenant does not have to answer questions, and we only need to see the rooms and features. If access is not possible, a Drive-By or Desktop report may be an option, and the report will state what was and was not observed.

Do you appraise duplexes and small multi-unit properties?

Yes, we appraise 2-4 unit properties. Pricing for the Desktop 2-4 Unit report begins at $549, and the 2-4 Unit Standard report, which includes an inspection, begins at $799. These reports look at comparable sales and, where it matters, market rents. Rent rolls, leases and expense information help, especially for estate and divorce work where income is part of the picture.

Do you appraise rural homes and acreage?

Yes, including properties in Valley Center, Ramona, Julian, Alpine, Jamul and the desert. Rural appraisals take more research because sales are fewer and features such as wells, septic, outbuildings, groves and road access all affect value. The fee may be higher than the starting price, but you will know the exact fee before anything is scheduled.

What if I think the report has a mistake?

Tell us. If a fact is wrong, such as square footage, bedroom count or a missed upgrade, send the correction with any support and Brian will review it. If the correction affects value, the report is revised. Disagreeing with the value alone is not a reason to change it, but new information or comparable sales Brian did not consider will always be reviewed.

How far do you travel?

We cover all of San Diego County, from Oceanside and Fallbrook down to San Ysidro, and east through Ramona, Julian, Campo and Borrego Springs. Very remote or complex assignments are reflected in the fixed quote, so there are no surprise charges afterward. Desktop reports can be done for any property in the county without a visit.
