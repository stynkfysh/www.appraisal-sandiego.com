Talmadge

1920s Spanish homes, decorative gates and quiet streets north of El Cajon Boulevard

Talmadge took its name from the silent film actresses Norma, Constance and Natalie Talmadge, who were linked to the original 1920s subdivision. Decorative gates still stand at the main entrances to the neighborhood, which lies north of El Cajon Boulevard, with Kensington on one side and the College Area on the other.

Most homes here are Spanish Revival and Craftsman houses from the 1920s through the 1940s, with some mid-century homes on the eastern side. Talmadge has a city maintenance assessment district that funds lighting and landscaping. For appraisal, the neighborhood's mix of styles and lot types means comps need to match closely.

The housing stock

Talmadge homes are mostly Spanish Colonial Revival and Craftsman bungalows from the 1920s to the 1940s, with mid-century ranch homes on the eastern side. Lots range from standard city lots to larger parcels near canyons. Postwar decades added some apartment buildings near El Cajon Boulevard. Condos are uncommon. Many homes have detached garages, and ADUs are becoming more common. The maintenance assessment district adds a small charge to property tax bills. Lots on the eastern side tend to be a little larger and flatter, and many houses there have been expanded over the years, so square footage and permit history vary more than the street view suggests.

Value drivers to watch around Talmadge

01

Spanish Revival homes

Talmadge buyers often look for original details like red tile roofs, arched windows, decorative ironwork and period bathrooms. Homes that kept or carefully restored those features tend to trade above remodels that removed them. We compare to homes with similar style, quality and level of updating.

02

Canyon lots

Homes on the northern streets near the canyons that drop toward Interstate 8 can have privacy and some views, but slope, drainage and fire hazard zoning can work against them. Insurance cost is part of what a buyer weighs. We compare to similar canyon-edge sales rather than to flat interior lots.

03

El Cajon Boulevard proximity

El Cajon Boulevard forms the southern edge and carries heavy traffic, buses and commercial uses. Homes within a block or two of it usually sell below similar homes deeper in the neighborhood on quiet curving streets. We look at block-level location and pick comps with similar exposure.

04

Maintenance assessment district

Talmadge's maintenance assessment district adds a modest charge to the property tax bill to pay for street lighting and landscaping. Most buyers treat it as a small cost for better-kept streets. We note it in the report and check that comparable sales carry similar charges.

Appraisals people order here

Older houses that have been owned for a long time frequently need estate, trust or date-of-death appraisals. Divorce appraisals come up for single-family homes. Family transfers and Proposition 19 planning are frequent. Pre-listing appraisals help sellers price restored Spanish Revival homes. A Desktop report can work for standard homes with plenty of sales. For restored homes, canyon lots or homes with ADUs, a Standard report with an inspection is usually better.

Asked about Talmadge

Does the Talmadge maintenance assessment district affect value?

The charge is modest and buyers usually see it as a small cost for well-kept streets. We note it in the report but rarely make a large adjustment.

Can you use Kensington sales as comps?

Yes, when they fit. Both neighborhoods have similar Spanish Revival homes. We adjust for location and lot differences.

How do you value a mid-century home in East Talmadge?

We compare to similar mid-century homes in Talmadge and nearby areas like the College Area, adjusting for size, condition and lot.
