Tierrasanta

A 1970s planned community on former Camp Elliott land beside Mission Trails

During World War II this land was part of Camp Elliott, a Marine training ground. Decades later, after the area was developed as a planned community starting in the early 1970s, leftover ordnance surfaced in the canyons and prompted a long federal cleanup. That history still comes up in disclosures, and it is worth knowing before you buy or appraise here.

Tierrasanta sits on a mesa bounded by Interstate 15, State Route 52 and Mission Trails Regional Park. It was planned with greenbelts, open space canyons, schools and a town center, and its Spanish-flavored street names reflect that design. Homes here range from small attached units to larger detached houses, and the open space around them affects both appeal and risk.

The housing stock

Most homes were built from the early 1970s through the 1980s. You will find detached single-family homes in a range of floor plans, often two stories, many around 1,400 to 2,500 square feet, on smaller lots typical of planned communities. There are also many townhomes and condos in clusters around the neighborhood. A good portion of the housing sits within homeowner associations, some with pools and shared greenbelts, while other tracts have no HOA. The neighborhood also has a maintenance assessment district for open space that can appear on property tax bills.

Value drivers to watch around Tierrasanta

01

Canyon and open space edges

Many homes back onto canyons or greenbelts, giving privacy and views. These locations are often in fire hazard severity zones, which affects brush clearance and insurance. The appraiser weighs the premium buyers pay for open space against those added costs.

02

HOA dues and amenities

Some tracts have HOAs with pools, landscaping and shared areas, while others have none. Dues, reserves and what the association covers affect what buyers will pay. Comparisons work best between homes with similar HOA structures. Gated pools and well-kept common areas help; high dues with thin reserves can hurt.

03

Ordnance history disclosures

The former military use of the land has led to cleanup work and disclosure requirements. Buyers generally treat it as background rather than a current hazard, but the appraisal should note any disclosures and reflect any measurable effect in sales.

04

Freeway and Miramar exposure

Homes close to Interstate 15 and State Route 52 deal with more traffic noise. Aircraft from MCAS Miramar can also be heard in parts of the neighborhood. Homes on interior streets away from these sources often sell higher than otherwise similar homes near them.

Appraisals people order here

Tierrasanta has a strong military and government workforce presence, and family transfers and divorce appraisals come up regularly. Original 1970s buyers are now handing homes to the next generation, and the number of inheritance-related valuations is climbing with it. Reports done before listing or before buying help sort out differences in HOAs and where each lot sits. Bankruptcy and immigration I-864 appraisals occasionally appear. A Desktop or Standard report works for most homes, with the Standard preferred when condition or canyon exposure is a major factor.

Asked about Tierrasanta

Does the old military history affect Tierrasanta home values?

Most buyers treat it as background information, and homes here sell steadily. The appraisal notes any required disclosures and checks whether nearby sales show any measurable effect. In most cases the impact on a specific home is small compared with location and condition.

How do HOA dues factor into my appraisal?

Higher dues increase a buyer's monthly cost, which can affect price, but they may also cover amenities and maintenance that add appeal. The appraiser compares homes with similar HOA situations whenever possible and notes the dues and any special assessments.

Can you appraise a Tierrasanta townhome with a Desktop report?

Yes, when the purpose allows and the unit's condition is known. Townhomes in the same complex often provide strong comparable sales. For a unit with a major remodel or one needing a lot of repair, a Standard report that includes an interior inspection tends to give a more dependable value.
