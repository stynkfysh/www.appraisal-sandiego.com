Encanto

Larger hillside lots and older homes in southeastern San Diego

Encanto feels different from much of southeastern San Diego. Its hilly streets, larger lots and older homes give it a semi-rural feel in places, with some properties still showing traces of earlier days when people kept gardens and small animals on their land. Streets climb and dip, sidewalks come and go, and two neighboring lots can differ in size by a factor of two or more. That irregularity is part of what makes valuing homes here interesting.

Encanto is the name of both a neighborhood and a larger city planning area, the Encanto Neighborhoods, that includes Emerald Hills, Valencia Park, Lincoln Park and others. The area borders Lemon Grove to the east and sits along the Orange Line trolley. Lot size and slope vary widely, which makes comparable selection and land value analysis important parts of any appraisal here.

The housing stock

Homes range from small 1920s and 1930s cottages to 1950s and 1960s ranch houses and some newer construction. Lots are often larger than in nearby neighborhoods, sometimes a quarter acre or more, and many are sloped. Some properties have extra dwellings, garages converted into living space or room for ADUs. Condition varies widely, from well-maintained and updated homes to those needing significant work. There are also some apartment buildings and condos, and a few newer infill subdivisions. Most detached homes have no HOA.

Value drivers to watch around Encanto

01

Lot size and ADU potential

Larger lots can support ADUs, second units or future development, which adds value. Buyers often pay more for flat, usable land. Steep lots may offer less usable space, so the appraiser considers both total and usable area. Access for construction and utility connections also matter for ADU potential.

02

Views from the hills

Some hillside homes in Encanto and Emerald Hills have views toward downtown, the bay or surrounding hills. Views can add value, and the appraiser compares with similar view properties nearby. Not every hillside lot has a good view, so the appraiser checks what the home actually sees.

03

Condition and updates

With homes spanning many decades, condition often drives the largest value differences. An updated home and one needing work can be very far apart in price. An interior inspection gives the clearest picture. Older systems such as wiring, plumbing and roofs are common concerns.

04

Transit and corridor exposure

Access to the Orange Line trolley and major roads like Imperial Avenue and Euclid Avenue can be a plus. Homes directly on busy streets may sell for less. The appraiser weighs convenience against traffic and noise. Quiet streets usually command more.

Appraisals people order here

As longtime owners leave their homes to heirs, estate-trust and date-of-death appraisals come up often. Family transfers come up frequently. Properties with extra units may call for a Desktop 2-4 Unit or Standard 2-4 Unit report. Pre-listing reports help sellers understand how lot size and ADU potential affect value. Divorce, bankruptcy and immigration I-864 appraisals also appear. Because condition and lot characteristics vary so much, a Standard report with a full inspection usually fits best.

Asked about Encanto

Does a large lot in Encanto add a lot of value?

It can, especially if the land is flat and could support an ADU or additional unit. Steep or hard-to-access land usually adds less. The appraiser looks at sales of similar lots to see how buyers have valued extra land. Usable size counts more than raw size.

How do you appraise a property with an ADU?

The appraiser looks at the ADU's size, condition and permit status and compares with sales of similar properties that have ADUs. If the property has two to four units, a multi-unit report type may be more appropriate. Unpermitted ADUs may get less credit.

Which comparables do you use for Encanto homes?

The closest and most similar sales, usually from Encanto and the surrounding Encanto Neighborhoods. If a property stands out, for example with an unusually big lot, the appraiser may look further afield to nearby areas like Lemon Grove. The report explains each choice.
