Bostonia

Older ranch homes and small lots on the northeast edge of El Cajon

Bostonia takes its name from settlers who came from Boston in the late 1800s and planted orchards and vineyards on the north side of the El Cajon Valley. The farms are long gone. What remains is a compact unincorporated area wedged between El Cajon city limits and Lakeside, with an El Cajon mailing address. Some long-time residents still use the old name, even though most maps fold the area into El Cajon.

You will find older single-family homes, some apartments and mobile home parks, and a few remaining larger lots that hint at the farm era. Because city and county boundaries zigzag through the area, it is worth checking exactly which jurisdiction a parcel falls in. That affects permits, zoning and sometimes how buyers see a property. That is the kind of detail a careful appraisal checks.

The housing stock

Bostonia's housing dates mostly from the late 1940s through the 1970s. Small ranch homes on quarter-acre or smaller lots dominate, many with stucco or wood siding and detached garages. A few older farmhouses remain on larger parcels. Duplexes, small apartment buildings and several mobile home parks add density along main roads such as Broadway, Second Street and Pepper Drive. A lot of homes have been remodeled or enlarged, and a few include an ADU or a garage conversion. Most are on public sewer and water. HOAs are rare outside of mobile home parks and a few newer infill developments.

Value drivers to watch around Bostonia

01

City versus county parcels

Bostonia straddles El Cajon city limits and unincorporated county land. Zoning, permit history and ADU rules can differ. The appraiser confirms jurisdiction and compares the home with sales under similar rules when it matters.

02

Age and condition

Much of the housing is 50 to 75 years old. Original plumbing, electrical and roofs affect value as much as square footage. The appraisal notes updates and condition, comparing to sales with similar levels of renovation.

03

Lot size and ADU potential

Larger leftover lots from the farm era can support additional units or garages. Buyers who see that potential may pay more. The report notes existing ADUs and compares with similar properties.

04

Nearby uses

Homes near commercial strips, busy roads or higher-density housing can sell at a discount to those on quiet residential streets. The appraiser checks location within the neighborhood against local sales.

Appraisals people order here

Bostonia produces regular trust and estate requests, along with date-of-death work for families settling older homes. Divorce and family-transfer appraisals are frequent. Some owners request pre-listing appraisals to set a realistic price on homes that need updating. Bankruptcy appraisals also come up. A Desktop report can work for planning on typical homes. A Standard appraisal with a full inspection fits better when condition, additions or court use matter. Small 2-4 unit properties here may call for a Desktop 2-4 Unit or Standard 2-4 Unit report.

Asked about Bostonia

Is Bostonia part of El Cajon?

Bostonia is an unincorporated community with an El Cajon mailing address, though city limits wind through the area. The appraiser checks which jurisdiction a parcel is in, since that can affect permits and zoning.

Can you appraise a Bostonia duplex or small multi-unit property?

Yes. Duplexes, triplexes and fourplexes are appraised using sales of similar small income properties. A Desktop 2-4 Unit starts at $549 and a Standard 2-4 Unit with inspection starts at $799.

How much does condition affect value in an older home?

A lot. On a single street, two homes of equal size can sell for very different prices because of how updated they are. The appraiser documents condition and compares with sales of similar quality, adjusting where needed.
