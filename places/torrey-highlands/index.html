Torrey Highlands

Newer homes along SR-56 between Peñasquitos and Carmel Valley

Torrey Highlands is a newer neighborhood in the City of San Diego, built mostly in the late 1990s and 2000s along State Route 56. It sits between Rancho Peñasquitos to the east, Carmel Valley to the west and Black Mountain Ranch to the north, with Camino Del Sur running through it.

Because the homes are newer and fairly similar, comparable sales are usually easy to find. But being on the edge of three different communities means you need to pick comparables carefully. A home in Torrey Highlands is not quite Carmel Valley and not quite PQ, and the market treats it accordingly. Commuters like the quick freeway access, and families like that the schools are in Poway Unified, which keeps demand steady.

The housing stock

Most homes in Torrey Highlands are two-story single-family houses built from the late 1990s through the 2000s, with stucco walls, tile roofs and Spanish or Mediterranean styles. Lots range from compact to medium. There are also some townhomes and condos. Many neighborhoods have HOAs, and community facilities district special taxes are common. Torrey Highlands Park and nearby open space add appeal. Some homes back to SR-56 or Camino Del Sur, while others back to canyons. Some later phases include larger plans with downstairs bedroom suites, three-car garages and bigger yards on the canyon edges. Interior condition varies more now than it did when the homes were new, since some have had two rounds of remodeling and others none.

Value drivers to watch around Torrey Highlands

01

Between three markets

Torrey Highlands borders Carmel Valley, Rancho Peñasquitos and Black Mountain Ranch, and prices differ among them. I use sales from Torrey Highlands first, then add carefully chosen sales from neighbors with similar homes, adjusting for location.

02

Special taxes

Many homes carry community facilities district taxes. Buyers consider total monthly cost, so a higher tax can slightly lower value. I check tax bills and compare homes with similar burdens.

03

Freeway frontage

Homes backing to SR-56 can be noisier. Homes backing to canyons or parks usually sell for more. I compare similar lot positions and adjust when needed.

04

Upgrades and condition

With homes 15 to 25 years old, some owners have remodeled and others have not. Kitchen and bath updates, flooring and solar affect value. I compare homes at similar update levels when possible.

Appraisals people order here

Torrey Highlands sees divorce, pre-purchase and pre-listing appraisals most often, as many families bought here in the 2000s. Estate-trust work is less common but does come up. Tax-appeal work sometimes arises when assessed values seem too high. Because homes are similar and sales are frequent, a Desktop or Basic Desktop report often works. For remodeled homes or those with unusual features, a Standard inspection is a better fit. Retrospective date-of-death and divorce values are also straightforward here, because sales data going back years is fairly deep for a neighborhood of this size.

Asked about Torrey Highlands

Is Torrey Highlands part of Carmel Valley?

No. It is its own community planning area, though it borders Carmel Valley. Prices often fall between Carmel Valley and Rancho Peñasquitos. I choose comparables accordingly.

Does backing to SR-56 lower value?

It often does, because of traffic noise. I compare homes with similar lot positions. When that is not possible, I make an adjustment based on market data.

What ZIP code is Torrey Highlands?

Torrey Highlands uses 92129, the same ZIP as Rancho Peñasquitos. I pick comparables by neighborhood, not just ZIP code, since homes and prices differ.
