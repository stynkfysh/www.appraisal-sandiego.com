San Elijo Hills

Master-planned hillside living under Double Peak, with the tax bill to match.

Up the long climb of San Elijo Road, past the last of the older San Marcos tracts, the hills open into a planned community built mostly from the early 2000s onward. San Elijo Hills is part of the City of San Marcos and shares its 92078 ZIP, but buyers shop it as its own market, and they compare it more often with other newer hillside communities than with the rest of the city.

Planners centered the community on a town core, its schools, and an extensive system of trails and open space, crowned by Double Peak Park. Homes are newer than most of North County, which makes condition differences smaller. The things that move value here are views, lot position, floor plan and the combined weight of HOA dues and Mello-Roos. Those deserve close attention in any appraisal.

The housing stock

Nearly everything in San Elijo Hills was built from about 2000 forward by several production builders, so you see repeated floor plans across neighborhoods: two-story single-family homes on compact lots, larger estate-style homes on the higher ridges, and attached townhomes and condos near the Town Center. Styles lean Spanish, Tuscan and Craftsman-influenced, with tile roofs and stucco. Nearly every home sits under a master HOA, many also have a sub-association, and most parcels fall within one or more Community Facilities Districts. Lots are terraced into the hillside, so slope banks and usable yard area vary widely from house to house even within one tract.

Value drivers to watch around San Elijo Hills

01

View and ridge position

Homes on the upper ridges can look west toward the ocean on clear days or across the valley toward Palomar. Lower homes may look at the house behind them. The same floor plan can sell at very different prices depending on view, so we judge views from inside the home.

02

Combined HOA and CFD cost

A master HOA, sometimes a sub-HOA, and Mello-Roos together can make the monthly carrying cost here higher than in older neighborhoods. Buyers do the math. We compare sales with similar tax and dues loads and note any CFD that has been paid down or will expire.

03

Usable yard on terraced lots

Hillside grading leaves some lots with large slope banks and small flat yards. A lot with a big level backyard, room for a pool or play area, is worth more than one that is mostly slope. We measure what is usable, not just the recorded lot size.

04

Fire setting and insurance

The community borders open space and chaparral, and parts fall in high fire hazard severity zones. Insurance availability and cost have become real factors for buyers here. We note the zone and any defensible-space or construction features that matter to the market.

Appraisals people order here

Because homes are relatively new and owners are often younger families, divorce appraisals are among the most frequent requests in San Elijo Hills. Pre-listing and pre-purchase reports also come up, as buyers and sellers try to price a specific floor plan against others in the same tract. Insurance-claim valuations after fire or water damage are another reason people call. The uniform housing stock means a Desktop report can work for a straightforward plan with plenty of sales, while view homes and upgraded estate lots usually call for a Standard inspection.

Asked about San Elijo Hills

With so many identical floor plans, why not just use a recent sale of the same model?

Same-model sales are a strong starting point, but upgrades, view, lot slope and street position can shift value a lot. A corner lot backing to a busy road and an interior lot on a quiet cul-de-sac are not equal, even with the same plan. We use same-plan sales when available, then adjust for the differences buyers here actually pay for.

Is San Elijo Hills appraised as part of San Marcos?

It is inside the San Marcos city limits and shares the 92078 ZIP, but we treat it as its own submarket. Comparables come from San Elijo Hills first, then from similar newer hillside communities nearby if the sales are thin, with the reasons for each choice written out in the report.

Does the special tax affect a divorce buyout value?

The appraisal gives market value, and the market already reflects CFD taxes. If comparables carry similar loads, no adjustment is needed. The tax itself is a separate number your attorneys or CPA may want to consider in the settlement. If anything about the property is unusual, tell us when you call so we can plan for it.
