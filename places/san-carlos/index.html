San Carlos

Homes below Cowles Mountain, between Lake Murray and Mission Trails

Cowles Mountain rises 1,593 feet over the east side of San Carlos, the highest point in the City of San Diego. On a weekend morning the trailhead lot is full. Along with the mountain, Lake Murray on the south side and Mission Trails Regional Park curving around the north and east are major reasons buyers choose this area. The neighborhood is quiet, mostly residential and well established.

San Carlos was built out largely from the late 1950s into the 1970s, so most homes are single-story ranch houses on slightly larger lots than you find closer to the city center. Patrick Henry High School anchors the neighborhood. The open space that makes the area appealing also brings fire zone and brush questions that a careful appraisal has to address.

The housing stock

The bulk of the housing is single-family homes built from about 1958 to 1975: three- and four-bedroom ranch houses, some split-levels, and a smaller number of two-story homes. Many are around 1,400 to 2,200 square feet on lots of 6,000 to 10,000 square feet, with larger lots on hillsides. Homes at the edges of Mission Trails and the slopes of Cowles Mountain may have views and direct trail access. There are a few condo and townhome complexes, generally near Navajo Road and Lake Murray Boulevard, with HOAs. Detached homes rarely have HOAs.

Value drivers to watch around San Carlos

01

Open space and trail access

Homes bordering Mission Trails or close to Cowles Mountain trailheads can attract buyers who want space and quiet. Backing onto open land usually adds value through privacy and views, though it also adds brush management and possibly wildlife concerns. The appraiser looks at whether the home actually backs onto the park or is simply nearby.

02

Fire hazard designation

Many streets near the open space fall within Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zones. That affects insurance options and cost and may require defensible-space clearance. The appraisal notes the designation and compares with homes that share similar exposure. Clearance zones of 100 feet or more can apply on canyon lots.

03

Lake Murray proximity

Homes near Lake Murray, especially those with lake views, draw interest from buyers looking for walking paths and water views. The premium depends on the view's quality and how close the home is to the lake's recreation areas. Homes across the street from the lake are a small, distinct group.

04

Lot size and pools

Lots here are generally larger than in many central neighborhoods, and pools are common. A usable flat yard, a pool in good condition or room for an ADU can add value, while a large sloped lot with little usable area may not.

Appraisals people order here

San Carlos sees a lot of estate and trust work, since many original owners and their families have held homes for decades. Date-of-death appraisals are especially common. Divorce appraisals arise in family homes near the schools. Pre-listing reports help sellers set a price, and pre-purchase appraisals can help buyers judge whether a home near open space is priced fairly given insurance costs. Insurance-claim appraisals sometimes come up near the fire zones. A Standard report is usually the right choice.

Asked about San Carlos

How does being near Mission Trails affect my home's value?

Bordering the open space often adds value through privacy, views and trail access. It can also bring fire zone requirements and insurance concerns. The appraisal looks at sales of similar homes near the park to see how buyers have weighed those factors.

Can my insurance situation affect the appraisal?

The appraisal does not use your policy, but market-wide insurance difficulty can affect what buyers will pay in fire-prone areas. If recent sales show that effect, it will be reflected in the comparable sales the appraiser uses. Your own premium does not set the value.

Do pools add value in San Carlos?

A pool in good condition on a usable lot can add value here, since many buyers expect one. An older pool needing repair may add little or even subtract. The appraiser compares with sales of similar homes with and without pools nearby.
