Normal Heights

Adams Avenue bungalows between I-805 and I-15, with canyon rims to the north

The neighborhood took its name from the State Normal School on Park Boulevard. Normal Heights stretches east along Adams Avenue from roughly Interstate 805 to Interstate 15, with the rim of Mission Valley as its northern edge and the area around El Cajon Boulevard to the south.

A 1985 brush fire raced up the canyon out of Mission Valley and destroyed dozens of houses on the north rim. Many of those lots were rebuilt with newer homes, so you will see a mix of 1920s bungalows and late 1980s construction there. For appraisal, that mix matters. Comps need to match era, size and lot, not just location.

The housing stock

Most homes are Craftsman and Spanish Revival bungalows built from about 1910 to 1940. Many sit on modest lots, often with alleys and detached garages. On the northern rim, houses that were rebuilt following the 1985 fire tend to be bigger and more recent. Postwar decades added stucco apartments, including Huffman six-packs. Adams Avenue has older commercial buildings, some with apartments above. Condos are less common than in Hillcrest. ADUs have become more common on alley lots.

Value drivers to watch around Normal Heights

01

Canyon rim location

Homes on the northern rim can look across Mission Valley, which buyers value. They also sit in fire hazard severity zones, and the 1985 fire showed what that means in practice. Insurance costs and availability can follow. We look at the view, slope, defensible space and recent rim sales before deciding how much the location adds or takes away.

02

Mixed construction eras

Homes rebuilt along the rim after the 1985 fire are newer and often larger than the 1920s bungalows a block or two south. Treating them as the same product would distort the value. We match comps by construction era and size first, and only mix eras when there is no other choice, with clear adjustments.

03

Adams Avenue proximity

Being within walking distance of Adams Avenue's restaurants, coffee shops and the annual street fair is a draw for many buyers. Houses right on Adams or on the busiest cross streets get more noise and parking competition. We look at the exact block and compare to sales with similar access.

04

ADU potential

Many Normal Heights lots have alleys, which makes a garage conversion or a new backyard unit easier to build. A permitted, rented ADU can add real value and widen the buyer pool. Unpermitted units may add little and can carry legal risk. We confirm permit status before giving the space full credit.

Appraisals people order here

Older bungalows that families have kept for decades often need estate-trust or date-of-death appraisals. Divorce appraisals come up for both houses and small multi-unit properties. Family transfers and Proposition 19 planning are frequent. Small apartment owners use 2-4 unit reports. Pre-purchase appraisals help buyers check prices on remodeled bungalows. A Desktop report often works for standard homes. For rim homes, remodeled properties or ADUs, a Standard report with an inspection is usually best.

Asked about Normal Heights

Does the 1985 fire still affect values on the canyon rim?

The fire history is part of why rim homes sit in fire hazard zones, which affects insurance costs. Views and newer construction can offset that. We compare to other rim sales to see how buyers treat it.

How do you compare a bungalow to a rebuilt home nearby?

We prefer to compare like to like: bungalows to bungalows, newer homes to newer homes. When we must mix, we adjust for age, size and quality.

Can you appraise a Huffman six-pack in Normal Heights?

Six units is more than our 2-4 unit reports cover, so it would need a different approach. For buildings with two to four units, we offer Desktop and Standard 2-4 Unit reports.
