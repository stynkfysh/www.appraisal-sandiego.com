Skyline

Hilltop tracts along Skyline Drive near Lemon Grove and Spring Valley

Skyline Drive follows the ridge here, and the name fits: homes along it and on nearby streets look out over southeastern San Diego, Lemon Grove and the hills to the east. The neighborhood, often called Skyline Hills, was built mostly in the 1950s and 1960s as tracts of single-family homes. From the higher streets you can see much of southeastern San Diego spread out below.

It sits between Encanto, Paradise Hills and the unincorporated communities of Spring Valley and La Presa. Long-term ownership is common. For an appraisal, the main questions are usually lot position on the hills, how a home has been updated since it was built and whether added living space was done with permits. Buyers here tend to be families, and many homes have been passed down within families too.

The housing stock

The typical home is a one-story ranch from the 1950s or 1960s with three or four bedrooms and roughly 1,100 to 1,600 sq. ft., on a lot of around 5,000 to 8,000 sq. ft. Hillside lots can be larger and more sloped. Many homes have additions, garage conversions or ADUs. There are also some townhomes and condo complexes from the 1970s and 1980s, some with HOAs, especially toward Bay Terraces. Most detached homes have no HOA. Condition ranges from fully remodeled to largely original.

Value drivers to watch around Skyline

01

Ridge and view lots

Homes on higher streets near Skyline Drive can have views across the valley and toward the hills or bay. Views usually add value, and the appraiser compares with similar view sales to measure the premium. Not every ridge home has an open view.

02

Additions and garage conversions

Many homes have added rooms or converted garages. Permitted space generally receives full value credit, while unpermitted space may receive less. Losing a garage can also affect value, since some buyers want covered parking. Permits make a difference. The appraiser checks this on site and in the sales data.

03

Condition and updating

Because many homes come from the same era and tracts, condition and updates drive most of the price differences. An interior inspection usually reveals the factors that matter most. Updated kitchens, baths and systems matter most. The appraiser checks this on site and in the sales data.

04

Corridor and county-line location

Homes near Jamacha Road or Skyline Drive get more traffic. The neighborhood borders unincorporated county areas, and some nearby comparables may be outside the city, so the appraiser selects them carefully. Proximity to schools and parks also helps. The appraiser checks this on site and in the sales data.

Appraisals people order here

Original buyers and their children have held many of these homes for decades, which means trust and inheritance valuations show up often. Family transfers come up frequently as well. Divorce and bankruptcy appraisals appear, and immigration I-864 appraisals sometimes arise when homeowners sponsor relatives. Pre-listing reports help sellers price homes with additions. A Standard report usually fits best because condition and additions vary so much, though a Desktop can work for well-documented homes or condos.

Asked about Skyline

Does a converted garage hurt my Skyline home's value?

It depends. If the conversion is permitted and well done, the added living space can add value, but losing covered parking may matter to some buyers. The appraiser compares with similar homes, both with and without conversions, to see how the market treats them.

Do you use Spring Valley or Lemon Grove sales as comparables?

Sometimes, if they are close and similar. The appraiser prefers sales within Skyline but may use nearby areas when needed and explains each choice in the report. County and city sales can behave a little differently. The report spells out the reasoning so you can follow it.

What report type works for an estate appraisal here?

An estate valuation usually calls for the Standard report with a walk-through, since condition at the moment of death feeds directly into the number. If the home is unavailable or condition is well documented, a Desktop may be acceptable depending on the purpose.
