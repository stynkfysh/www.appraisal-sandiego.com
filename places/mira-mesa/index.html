Mira Mesa

1970s tract homes on a mesa between canyons and an air station

By population, Mira Mesa ranks among San Diego's biggest communities; it covers a wide mesa bounded by Los Peñasquitos Canyon on the north, crossed by Carroll Canyon on the south, with Marine Corps Air Station Miramar off to the southeast. It grew quickly in the 1970s, when builders put up thousands of affordable tract homes.

Those homes are now around 50 years old, and their condition varies a lot. Location within Mira Mesa also matters, especially distance from the air station's flight paths. Newer development in Carroll Canyon, including the 3Roots neighborhood, has added a different kind of housing to the mix. For you, that means a single ZIP code holds very different properties, and the comparable search has to respect those lines.

The housing stock

Mira Mesa's housing mostly went up in the 1970s and the early part of the 1980s: one- and two-story tract houses finished in stucco or wood siding, typically on 5,000 to 7,000 square foot lots. Many have been updated or expanded. There are also condos and townhomes, especially along Mira Mesa Boulevard and Camino Ruiz. Newer homes and townhomes have been built in Carroll Canyon, including 3Roots, with HOAs and Mello-Roos taxes. Older tracts usually have no HOA. ADUs and garage conversions are increasingly common. Expect older electrical panels, single-pane windows and original plumbing in homes that have not been updated.

Value drivers to watch around Mira Mesa

01

Air station noise

Homes closer to MCAS Miramar flight paths can see a discount. Noise varies across the community. I check noise contour maps and compare homes with similar exposure when the sales are available.

02

Condition of older tracts

Fifty-year-old homes vary from original to fully remodeled. Updated kitchens, baths, windows and systems all matter. I rate condition carefully and choose comparables at similar update levels.

03

ADUs and rental potential

Mira Mesa's proximity to Sorrento Valley jobs and UC San Diego makes rental demand strong. Permitted ADUs and extra bedrooms can add value. Unpermitted space counts less. I check permit records.

04

Newer construction in Carroll Canyon

Homes in 3Roots and other new developments differ from older tracts in age, style and tax burden. I compare them with other new construction rather than with 1970s homes.

Appraisals people order here

Mira Mesa sees many date-of-death, estate-trust and family-transfer appraisals because many original owners are still in their homes. Divorce appraisals are common too. Bankruptcy and immigration I-864 appraisals come up occasionally, when a household needs a supported value for court or a sponsorship filing. Pre-listing appraisals help sellers of dated homes price them realistically. A Desktop report works for many tract homes with good sales nearby. A Standard inspection is better for homes with ADUs, additions or significant remodels.

Asked about Mira Mesa

Does an ADU add value to a Mira Mesa home?

A permitted ADU usually adds value, especially given rental demand. How much depends on size, quality and how the market has treated similar homes. Unpermitted units contribute less and may raise concerns for buyers.

How do you account for MCAS Miramar noise?

I check where the home sits relative to published noise contours and compare it with sales that have similar exposure. If that is not possible, I make an adjustment based on market data.

Can you do an I-864 appraisal for a Mira Mesa home?

Yes. An I-864 appraisal supports the value of a home used as an asset for immigration sponsorship. A Desktop report often works if the home is typical and recent sales are available.
