Carmel Mountain Ranch

1980s and 1990s planned community, condos to detached homes, east of I-15

Carmel Mountain Ranch sits between Interstate 15 and the hills to the east, just south of Rancho Bernardo. It was planned in the 1980s and built out mostly by the late 1990s. A big shopping center, Carmel Mountain Plaza, sits at its western edge, and for years a golf course ran through the middle of the community.

That golf course closed, and its future became one of the bigger local real estate stories. For homeowners whose yards faced the fairways, the question of what replaces the open space has a direct effect on how buyers see their homes. It is one of several factors I watch closely here.

The housing stock

Housing in Carmel Mountain Ranch was built mostly from the mid-1980s through the late 1990s. You will find detached single-family homes, many two-story with tile roofs, along with a large share of condos and townhomes. Lots are typically small to medium. Highland Ranch, on the north side, has its own collection of tracts. HOAs are common, with many homes under both a master and sub-association. Condo complexes vary in age, amenities and dues. Several neighborhoods back to the former golf course or to hillside open space.

Value drivers to watch around Carmel Mountain Ranch

01

Former golf course frontage

Part of what buyers paid for homes that used to back onto the golf course was the view. With the course closed and redevelopment plans in play, buyers now weigh what will be built there. I look at recent sales of former fairway homes, not old golf-era sales.

02

Condo HOA health

For condos and townhomes, monthly dues, reserves and any pending special assessments matter. Higher dues lower what buyers can pay. I compare units in complexes with similar dues and amenities when possible.

03

Freeway access and noise

Quick access to Interstate 15 and SR-56 is a plus for commuters. Homes right along the freeway or near the commercial area can see some discount for noise and traffic. I compare similar lot positions first.

04

Age of systems

Many homes here are 30 to 40 years old. Original roofs, windows, HVAC and plumbing are reaching the end of their life. Homes with updated systems sell better. I note what has been replaced when I can confirm it.

Appraisals people order here

In Carmel Mountain Ranch, condo and townhome appraisals are common for estate-trust, date-of-death and divorce work. Family-transfer appraisals come up when a parent sells a condo to a child. Pre-listing appraisals help sellers of former golf course homes set a realistic price. Bankruptcy appraisals sometimes come up on condos where a fair value is needed for court. A Desktop report works well for many condos and standard homes with good comparable sales. A Standard inspection is a better fit for remodeled homes or properties with questions about condition.

Asked about Carmel Mountain Ranch

How does the old golf course affect my home's value?

It depends on your lot and on what buyers expect will be built there. I look at recent sales of homes that backed to the former course and compare them with similar homes elsewhere in the community. That shows how the market is treating them now.

Do condo HOA dues affect the appraised value?

Yes. Higher dues reduce what buyers can afford, so they usually lower value somewhat. I compare units with similar dues and also note reserves or special assessments when that information is available.

Is Highland Ranch part of Carmel Mountain Ranch?

Highland Ranch is a set of tracts on the north side of the Carmel Mountain Ranch area. I consider sales from both, but adjust for differences in age, home size and lot type.
