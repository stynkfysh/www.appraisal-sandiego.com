Barrio Logan

Chicano Park, shipyards and old cottages beneath the Coronado Bridge

Under the Coronado Bridge, the concrete pillars of Chicano Park are covered in murals. The park became a National Historic Landmark in 2017, and it stands at the center of a neighborhood that has fought for decades to keep housing next to the shipyards and industry along San Diego Bay. Weekend visitors come for the murals, galleries and restaurants along Logan Avenue.

Barrio Logan is small, wedged between Interstate 5, the bay and the Navy and shipbuilding facilities to the south. Homes sit beside auto shops, warehouses and art studios. That mix means appraisals here involve more than square footage. Zoning, nearby land uses and the condition of very old homes all play a part in what a property is worth. Few sales happen in a given year.

The housing stock

Housing is a mix of small wood-frame cottages and homes from the early 1900s through the 1940s, some Victorian-era structures, and apartment buildings and newer mixed-use and affordable housing projects. Lots are often small and narrow. Many homes have been modified over the decades, and some have additional units on the same lot. Condition varies widely. Newer condos and lofts exist in a few mixed-use buildings, with HOAs. Many properties sit within zoning that allows a mix of residential and commercial or industrial uses.

Value drivers to watch around Barrio Logan

01

Zoning and land use

Properties may fall under zoning that allows commercial or industrial uses alongside housing, and the community plan has been the subject of long-running debate. Zoning can affect what a buyer can build or do with a lot, so the appraiser checks it carefully.

02

Industrial neighbors

Homes next to shipyards, auto shops or freeway ramps deal with noise, traffic and air quality concerns. These often sell for less than homes on more residential blocks. Location within the neighborhood matters as much as the home itself. A home on a quiet residential block may sell well above one beside a busy truck route.

03

Age and condition

Many homes are over a century old and may need foundation, electrical or plumbing work. Others have been fully renovated. An interior inspection usually reveals the biggest value differences between similar-looking homes. Original wood siding, porches and trim can add appeal when they are in good repair.

04

Multiple units on a lot

Some properties have more than one dwelling on a single lot, which can add income potential. Permit status and condition of each unit matter. Two-to-four-unit properties may require a report designed for multi-unit valuation. Income from each unit and its condition are both considered.

Appraisals people order here

Estate-trust and date-of-death appraisals come up as long-time family owners pass homes to heirs. Family transfers are also common within families that have lived here for generations. Properties with multiple units may need a Desktop 2-4 Unit or Standard 2-4 Unit report. Pre-listing reports help owners understand how zoning and nearby uses affect price. Bankruptcy and divorce appraisals appear as well. Since the homes here vary so much in age and style, the right call is usually a Standard report that includes an interior inspection.

Asked about Barrio Logan

How does zoning affect my Barrio Logan property's value?

Zoning determines what can legally be built or operated on the lot. Properties with flexible zoning may attract buyers interested in redevelopment, while others may be limited. The appraiser reviews the zoning and considers how it affects comparable sales. Mixed-use zoning can widen the buyer pool.

Can you appraise a lot with two houses on it?

Yes. A property with two to four units usually calls for a Desktop 2-4 Unit or Standard 2-4 Unit report, which looks at both the market value and any rental income. Permit status of each unit is noted in the report.

Do nearby shipyards and freeways lower values?

They can. Homes close to heavy industry or freeway ramps often sell for less than homes on quieter blocks. To measure that impact, the appraiser searches for comparable sales in similar settings. The effect varies with distance, traffic, noise and the surrounding block, so the report compares like with like.
