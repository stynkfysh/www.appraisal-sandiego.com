Downtown San Diego

High-rise condos, lofts and row homes between the bay and Interstate 5

If you own property downtown, odds are it is a condo. The neighborhood holds most of the city's residential towers, and its housing runs from 1990s mid-rises in the Marina district to newer glass towers in East Village and along the waterfront. Downtown is split into districts: the Gaslamp Quarter, East Village, Cortez Hill, Columbia, Core, the Marina and Little Italy, which gets its own page here.

Appraising downtown is mostly about separating one unit from the units around it. Floor level, view direction, parking spaces, HOA dues and the building's age and reserves all move value. Two units of the same size in the same tower can be far apart in price if one faces the bay and the other faces a parking structure or a new building going up next door. We compare within the building first and then with similar buildings nearby.

The housing stock

Most homes downtown are condominiums in mid-rise and high-rise buildings, with a smaller supply of lofts in converted warehouses and brick buildings around the Gaslamp and East Village. The Marina district has older condo towers and a run of two- and three-story townhomes and row homes near Columbia Street and the waterfront parks. Cortez Hill has some older apartments and a few surviving early 1900s houses. East Village has seen the most new construction since Petco Park opened in 2004. Nearly every property sits in an HOA, and dues commonly cover security, elevators, amenities and insurance on the building.

Value drivers to watch around Downtown San Diego

01

View lines and what blocks them

A clear bay or skyline view is often the biggest single difference between units. Downtown keeps growing, and a view sold today can be partly blocked by a tower that breaks ground next year. We note which direction a unit faces, how high it is and whether nearby parcels are approved for development, then compare against sales with similar exposure.

02

HOA dues and building health

High dues reduce what a buyer can pay each month, so they pull on value. So does a weak reserve fund or a pending special assessment. We review the dues, look for any known assessments or litigation in the documents you give us, and compare to sales in buildings with similar costs and amenities.

03

Parking and storage

Deeded parking spaces are scarce in older downtown buildings, and some units sell with none. A unit with two deeded spaces or a private storage room can trade noticeably higher. We confirm what is assigned to the unit and adjust comps that differ.

04

Noise and street level

Units near the ballpark, trolley lines, freeways or the Gaslamp bar blocks hear more at night. Lower floors facing a busy street usually trade below quieter interior units in the same building. We account for location within the building as well as the building itself.

Appraisals people order here

Divorce files here frequently involve a downtown condo, where one spouse keeps the unit, and in estate-trust and date-of-death work when an owner passes and heirs need a stepped-up basis. Investors who hold units as rentals ask for values for partnership buyouts, family transfers and tax planning. Pre-listing appraisals help sellers price a unit against the other listings in the same building. For a condo with plenty of in-building sales, a Desktop report can be enough. If the unit is remodeled or damaged, you are better served by a Standard report that includes an interior inspection.

Asked about Downtown San Diego

Can you use sales from my own building?

Yes, and we prefer to. Sales in the same tower share the same HOA, amenities and location, so they need fewer adjustments. When there are not enough recent sales in your building, we add sales from similar towers nearby with comparable age, dues and views.

Does a downtown condo need an interior inspection?

Not always. If the unit is close to original condition and the building has recent sales, a Desktop report often supports a solid value. Remodeled kitchens, upgraded finishes, water damage or an unusual floor plan are good reasons to choose a Standard report so the interior is seen and documented.

How do you handle a unit with a special assessment pending?

We note it and consider how buyers treat it. Often the seller pays it or the price drops by roughly the amount owed, but that depends on timing and the amount. If you have the HOA notice, send it with the order so the report reflects it.
