San Ysidro

Older bungalows and small rentals beside the busiest land border crossing

San Ysidro got its start in 1909, when the Little Landers colony set up a small farm settlement founded on the belief that a family could support itself on a small plot. The City of San Diego annexed it in 1957. Today it sits right at the San Ysidro Port of Entry, where Interstate 5 and Interstate 805 meet the border, and the Blue Line trolley ends a few steps from the crossing.

Housing here is older, denser and more rental-heavy than most of the South Bay. Many owners have lived in the area for generations, and plenty of lots hold more than one unit. That makes local knowledge important. A house on a quiet street near Larsen Field and one a block off the freeway can sell quite differently, and the appraisal should catch that.

The housing stock

The core of San Ysidro, west of Interstate 805 and north of the border, has small early 20th-century and post-war bungalows, 1950s and 1960s stucco homes and many duplexes and small apartment buildings. Lots tend to be modest, and second units are common. East of Interstate 805 along Beyer Boulevard and nearby streets, housing includes 1970s and 1980s tracts, condos and townhome complexes, plus some newer infill. Several larger apartment complexes and mobile home parks round out the stock. HOAs apply mainly to condos and townhomes.

Value drivers to watch around San Ysidro

01

Multiple units and rents

Many San Ysidro lots hold two or more units. For two to four units, value depends on rents as well as sales. Leases, rent amounts and permit records for each unit help the appraisal reflect what an investor or owner-occupant would actually pay.

02

Freeway and port proximity

Homes right next to Interstate 5, Interstate 805 or the port area see noise and traffic, and some buyers discount for that. A few blocks away, the effect is smaller. The report compares sales with similar exposure or adjusts for the difference.

03

Age and condition

Much of the housing is 50 to 100 years old. Some homes have been fully updated, others have original systems. Condition, roof, plumbing, electrical and any additions weigh heavily, which is why an interior inspection usually tells the real story here.

04

Unpermitted additions

In the older South Bay neighborhoods you will often see garage conversions, enclosed patios and added rooms. Permitted space counts as living area. Unpermitted space is noted and usually valued at less. The appraisal is clear about which is which.

Appraisals people order here

Immigration I-864 appraisals are among the most frequent requests in San Ysidro, since many families sponsor relatives and use home equity to qualify. Estate-trust and date-of-death work is common for homes held for decades. Family transfers come up often, and bankruptcy and bail-bond appraisals appear too. Many properties have more than one unit, so the Desktop 2-4 Unit at $549 or Standard 2-4 Unit at $799 may apply. For a single-family I-864 file, a Desktop report at $449 is often enough. Divorce work usually calls for a Standard inspection.

Asked about San Ysidro

What do I need for an I-864 appraisal on my San Ysidro home?

A current appraisal showing market value, plus your mortgage balance, so equity can be calculated. A Desktop report at $449 often works. Your immigration attorney can confirm what format they prefer. Reports are delivered as a PDF you can attach to your filing.

My San Ysidro lot has two houses. How is it appraised?

If both are legal units, it is appraised as a 2-4 unit property, using sales and rents of similar properties. If one is not permitted, the lot is usually appraised as a single-family home and the extra structure is described separately.

Can you appraise a San Ysidro home for a family member buying it from me?

Yes. Family-transfer appraisals help document a fair market price for tax and estate purposes. A Standard appraisal with full inspection gives the strongest support, but a Desktop report can work when the home's condition is typical and not in dispute.
