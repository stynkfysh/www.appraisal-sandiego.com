San Pasqual Valley

City-owned farmland, scattered private homes and wells east of Lake Hodges

Most people do not realize the San Pasqual Valley is inside the City of San Diego. It sits east of Lake Hodges and south of Escondido, and its mailing addresses say Escondido. Much of the valley floor is city-owned agricultural preserve, leased to farms, dairies and nurseries. The San Diego Zoo Safari Park and San Pasqual Battlefield State Historic Park are both here.

Private homes are scattered along the valley edges and side roads. They are often on large lots with wells and septic systems, and they rarely come up for sale. That makes appraising them a different job from valuing a tract home in the suburbs. Access roads, easements and what the land can legally be used for all deserve attention before any value is set.

The housing stock

Housing in the San Pasqual Valley is limited and varied. You will find older ranch homes, farmhouses, manufactured homes and some newer custom homes on parcels from about one acre to many acres. The majority depend on septic systems, and water comes from a private well or a shared system. Some parcels include barns, greenhouses, orchards or other agricultural improvements. There are no large subdivisions in the valley itself, though nearby areas like Rancho Bernardo and Escondido have tract housing. HOAs are rare.

Value drivers to watch around San Pasqual Valley

01

Wells and septic

Water supply and waste systems are critical here. Well production, water quality and septic capacity all affect value. I note the systems in the report and recommend inspections where needed.

02

Agricultural use

Some parcels have orchards, greenhouses or pasture. These improvements can add value to the right buyer. I look at how similar agricultural properties have sold and how much the improvements contribute.

03

Thin sales data

Private sales in the valley are rare. I often use sales from rural Escondido, Ramona or Valley Center with similar land and improvements, then adjust for differences in location and access.

04

Fire and flood exposure

Hillside parcels can be in high fire hazard areas, and some valley-floor land is near the San Dieguito River floodplain. Both affect insurance and buyer interest. I check hazard maps and report the results.

Appraisals people order here

In the San Pasqual Valley, the requests I get most are estate-trust and date-of-death appraisals, because a lot of properties have stayed with one family for decades. Family-transfer appraisals come up when land passes to the next generation. Divorce and tax-appeal work also occur. Because properties are rural and unique, a Standard appraisal with a full inspection is almost always the right choice. Larger acreage and agricultural properties may be quoted higher due to complexity. I also check zoning, any agricultural lease terms that affect the private parcel, and road access before setting comparables.

Asked about San Pasqual Valley

Is San Pasqual Valley in Escondido or San Diego?

The valley is within City of San Diego limits, but it uses Escondido mailing addresses and ZIP codes 92025 and 92027. Much of the land is city-owned and leased for farming.

How do you value a San Pasqual Valley property with few sales?

I look further in time and area, using rural sales from Escondido, Ramona and Valley Center with similar land and improvements. I adjust for differences and explain each choice.

Do wells and septic affect value?

Yes. A reliable well and a properly sized septic system support value. Problems with either can reduce it. I note the systems in the report and recommend inspections when there are concerns.
