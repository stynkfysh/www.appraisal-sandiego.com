Point Loma

Bay views, Navy history and flight paths on one long peninsula

Point Loma is a peninsula, and that shapes almost everything about its real estate. San Diego Bay lies on one side, the ocean on the other, and the ridge between them gives many homes a view of one or both. The community uses ZIP 92106 on the bay side and 92107 for Point Loma Heights and Sunset Cliffs, while the Navy and Cabrillo National Monument hold the southern tip.

Neighborhoods here have distinct identities. La Playa and the Wooded Area are older, established and expensive. Fleetridge and Roseville sit on the slopes above Shelter Island. Loma Portal lies under the airport's departure path near Liberty Station, the former Naval Training Center that is now a mixed-use district. Each pocket pulls its own set of comparable sales, and borrowing across them takes care.

The housing stock

The oldest homes are around Roseville and La Playa, where early 1900s Craftsman and Spanish Revival houses mix with mid-century rebuilds. The Wooded Area, on the ridge among pines and eucalyptus, is known for 1950s and 60s custom ranch and modern homes on larger, irregular lots. Fleetridge and Point Loma Heights have many post-war homes, often remodeled or raised to a second story for views. Loma Portal has 1920s through 40s homes on flatter streets. Condos concentrate near Shelter Island, along Rosecrans Street and at Liberty Station, where townhomes went up in the 2000s. Most of the peninsula is single-family, with few large tracts and plenty of one-off custom houses.

Value drivers to watch around Point Loma

01

Bay and ocean views

A ridge home looking over the bay, the downtown skyline and Coronado sells in a different bracket from one on the same street without that view. Views are verified from inside the house. Adjustments come from paired sales on the peninsula itself, since view premiums here do not match those in other coastal neighborhoods.

02

Airport noise in Loma Portal

Departures from San Diego International pass over Loma Portal and nearby streets. Homes in the higher noise contours may have received sound insulation through the airport's Quieter Home Program, and they fall within the airport land use compatibility plan. Noise is a real value factor, so the appraisal uses sales with similar exposure.

03

Lot shape and topography

Point Loma lots are rarely uniform. Steep slopes, odd shapes, private lanes and canyon edges are common, especially in the Wooded Area and Fleetridge. A large lot that is mostly hillside may add less than its size suggests. The appraisal looks at usable area, access and build potential, not just land square footage.

04

Custom homes, thin comparables

With few large tracts, many homes are one of a kind. That makes comparable selection harder and often means reaching further back in time or across the peninsula. The report explains every comparable choice and every adjustment, so a reader can follow the logic from start to finish.

Appraisals people order here

Because so many Point Loma homes are owned for a long time, estate, trust and date-of-death appraisals come up often, typically valued back to the day a parent died. Divorce appraisals come up for custom homes where the value range is wide and both sides need an independent number. Pre-listing appraisals help on unusual properties with few direct comparables, and family-transfer work comes up when homes pass between generations. Because the homes vary so much, a Standard $699 with interior inspection is the usual fit. View homes and large lots may be quoted higher, with the fee set before scheduling.

Asked about Point Loma

Does airport noise lower values in Loma Portal?

Noise is one factor among several. Loma Portal homes are compared mainly with other homes under similar flight paths, so the noise effect is already built into those sales. If the subject sits in a noticeably louder or quieter spot than the comparables, the appraisal adjusts for it and explains why.

How do you appraise a custom home in the Wooded Area?

Start with sales in the Wooded Area and similar ridge-top neighborhoods, then adjust for size, view, lot usability, quality and condition. When direct sales are thin, the appraisal may reach back further in time and apply a market-conditions adjustment, which is stated and supported in the report.

Which ZIP is my Point Loma home in?

Most of the bay side, including La Playa, Roseville, Fleetridge and the Wooded Area, uses 92106. Point Loma Heights and Sunset Cliffs are generally 92107, shared with Ocean Beach. For comparables, the micro-neighborhood matters more than the ZIP, so sales are chosen by street and setting first.
