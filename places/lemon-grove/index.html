Lemon Grove

Small city, big lemon, and lots with room for a second unit

There is a giant lemon on Broadway, and it has been there since 1928. It is a reminder that this was citrus country before it was suburbs. Lemon Grove incorporated in 1977 and covers under four square miles, wedged between La Mesa, Spring Valley and the City of San Diego, with State Routes 94 and 125 and the Orange Line trolley crossing through.

Being small does not make it uniform. You will find 1920s farmhouses next to 1950s tracts, flat lots near the trolley and sloped lots in the southern hills. If you need a value for an estate, a family transfer or a sale, the useful comparables are often a few blocks away rather than across the whole city, and sometimes they come from neighboring La Mesa or Spring Valley.

The housing stock

Lemon Grove's housing is mostly single-family homes on lots that tend to run larger than typical San Diego tracts, a holdover from the orchard days. Post-war ranch homes from the 1940s through 1960s make up the bulk. Scattered among them are older farmhouses and cottages, some on lots big enough to keep animals or add a second dwelling. Monterey Heights and other hillside areas in the south have sloped lots and some views. Small apartment buildings and a few condo complexes sit near Broadway and the trolley stations, and newer townhome projects have appeared near the Lemon Grove Depot area.

Value drivers to watch around Lemon Grove

01

Big lots and ADUs

Many Lemon Grove lots have room for an accessory dwelling unit, and some already have one. A permitted ADU with its own entrance adds value that buyers and investors will pay for. An unpermitted granny flat is handled carefully in the report, since the value it adds depends on its legal status.

02

Freeway and trolley proximity

State Routes 94 and 125 and the trolley tracks make commuting easy, but homes right against them pick up noise. Brian compares sales near the corridors with sales on interior streets to see whether an adjustment is warranted and how large it should be.

03

Mixed-age blocks

A 1925 farmhouse, a 1955 ranch and a 2010 rebuild can share one street. Comparing by age and condition, not just square footage, keeps the analysis honest. Older homes may need foundation, wiring or roof work that buyers price in.

04

Hillside lots in the south

Sloped lots in Monterey Heights and nearby streets can have views, but also steep driveways and less usable yard. Those trade-offs push value in both directions. The report weighs usable land, not just total lot size.

Appraisals people order here

Family-transfer appraisals come up often in Lemon Grove, where homes pass between generations and Proposition 19 rules make the value at transfer important for property tax. Date-of-death and estate-trust work is common for the same reason. Pre-purchase appraisals help buyers looking at homes with ADUs or large lots decide whether the asking price holds up. A Desktop report fits many ordinary ranch homes. If the property has a second unit, it may be a 2-4 unit property, which calls for the Desktop 2-4 Unit or Standard 2-4 Unit report.

Asked about Lemon Grove

My Lemon Grove lot has a house and a permitted ADU. Which report do I need?

A single-family home with one ADU is still usually appraised as a single-family property, with the ADU valued as a feature. If there are two or more separate legal units, the 2-4 Unit reports apply. Send the permits and Brian will confirm which fits before quoting.

Can you use La Mesa or Spring Valley sales for my Lemon Grove home?

Yes, when they are close and similar. Lemon Grove is small, so the best comparables sometimes sit just over the city line. Brian explains in the report why each sale was chosen and adjusts for any location difference.

I am transferring my house to my son. Why do I need an appraisal?

Under Proposition 19, a parent-to-child transfer may keep part of the low assessed value only up to a limit tied to market value. An appraisal documents that value as of the transfer date. Your CPA or attorney uses it to work out the tax effect.
