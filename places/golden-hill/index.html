Golden Hill

Victorian-era homes and downtown views on the east side of Balboa Park

Golden Hill is one of San Diego's oldest suburbs, laid out during the land boom of the late 1880s. Wealthy families built large homes here with views west over the city and the bay. Many of those Victorian and early Craftsman houses still stand, mixed with later apartments and bungalows.

The neighborhood sits between Balboa Park and State Route 94, just east of downtown. Its location means some homes have strong downtown or bay views. Its age means condition varies a lot. A carefully restored Queen Anne and a converted rooming house on the same block can have very different values, so we look closely at each property's history and current state.

The housing stock

Golden Hill has Victorian homes, including Queen Anne and Stick style, from the 1880s and 1890s, plus Craftsman and Prairie houses from the early 1900s and Spanish Revival homes from the 1920s. Some large historic homes were divided into apartments over the years. Postwar construction added stucco apartment buildings. The neighborhood also has condos and townhomes, many in older converted buildings or smaller new projects. Lot sizes vary, and many homes sit on hillsides with views. The 25th Street and Broadway area has older commercial buildings.

Value drivers to watch around Golden Hill

01

Downtown and bay views

Homes on the western slopes of Golden Hill can look straight out at the downtown skyline, the bay and Coronado. A wide, protected view from the main living spaces adds real value here, while a partial view from an upstairs window adds less. We document what you can actually see and compare to sales with similar views.

02

Condition of century-old homes

Many houses here are more than 100 years old. You will find a wide range of condition in foundations, roofs, original knob-and-tube wiring and galvanized pipes. A buyer facing a new foundation or a full rewire will pay less, and a house that has had those done is worth more. We look at condition carefully and compare to homes in similar shape.

03

Converted single-family homes

Over the decades, some large Victorians were split into apartments or rooming houses. Whether those units are permitted, how well they were done, and whether converting back to a single home makes sense all affect value. We check available permit history and value the property as it legally stands.

04

Freeway and park edges

Homes along the southern edge near State Route 94 can deal with freeway noise and traffic. Homes near Golden Hill Park, the golf course and the eastern edge of Balboa Park benefit from the open space. A few blocks can make a measurable difference, so we pick comps with similar surroundings.

Appraisals people order here

Estate-trust and date-of-death appraisals come up often for older homes held for a long time. Divorce appraisals cover both single-family homes and condos. Family transfers and Proposition 19 planning are common. Owners of converted homes or small multi-unit buildings use 2-4 unit reports. Insurance-claim valuations sometimes follow damage to older homes. A Standard report with an inspection is usually right, since age, condition and conversions need careful review.

Asked about Golden Hill

How do you value a Victorian home in Golden Hill?

We look for sales of similar older homes in Golden Hill and nearby areas, adjusting for size, condition, view and restoration quality. We note historic features and any designation.

My home was divided into apartments. How is it appraised?

If it has two to four legal units, we use the 2-4 unit approach, looking at sales and rents. If units are unpermitted, we note that and consider how a buyer would treat them.

Does freeway noise from State Route 94 matter?

For homes close to the freeway, yes. We compare to sales with similar exposure.
