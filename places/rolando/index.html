Rolando

Prewar and postwar bungalows along University Avenue at the La Mesa line

Rolando lies on the City of San Diego's eastern boundary next to La Mesa, just south of the College Area. It is a small neighborhood, mostly residential, with a commercial strip along University Avenue that the local business improvement district calls Rolando Village. It has a quieter, more settled feel than the streets closer to campus, with long-time owners and families on most blocks. The Green Line trolley stops at 70th Street, just north of the neighborhood, and Interstate 8 is a short drive north.

Homes here are older than in many nearby areas, with many built in the 1940s and 1950s and a few from earlier decades. Rolando Park, south of University Avenue, adds a set of postwar ranch homes on hillier ground. That variety of eras and styles means that comparable sales need to be chosen carefully, matching age and character, not just size.

The housing stock

The housing stock includes small bungalows, minimal traditional homes and Spanish-style houses from the late 1930s through the early 1950s, along with ranch homes from the 1950s and 1960s, especially in Rolando Park. A typical one has roughly 900 to 1,600 square feet and sits on a lot near 5,000 to 7,000 square feet. Some homes have been remodeled or expanded, others are close to original. There are a few small apartment buildings and condos, mostly near University Avenue. Most detached homes have no HOA.

Value drivers to watch around Rolando

01

Age and character

Older Spanish-style and bungalow homes with original details can attract buyers who value character, while plain postwar homes may draw a different buyer. Matching age and architectural style when selecting comparables helps produce a more accurate value. A 1940s cottage should be compared first with other prewar homes.

02

Hillside lots in Rolando Park

Homes in Rolando Park often sit on sloped lots, some with views toward the hills. Views add value, but slopes can limit usable yard space and complicate additions or ADUs. The appraisal weighs both. Retaining walls and drainage also need attention on steeper sites.

03

Proximity to SDSU

Rolando is close enough to San Diego State University to draw some rental demand, though less than the College Area. Homes with extra bedrooms can attract investors, while owner-occupied homes on quiet streets appeal to families. The appraiser considers both groups when choosing comparable sales.

04

University Avenue exposure

Homes near University Avenue get more traffic and noise but are closer to shops and restaurants. Those on quiet interior streets often sell higher. Location within the neighborhood matters in comparable selection. Walkable access to coffee shops and restaurants along the avenue is a plus for some buyers.

Appraisals people order here

Rolando sees a steady flow of estate-trust and date-of-death appraisals as long-term owners pass homes to heirs. Family transfers are also common. Pre-listing and pre-purchase reports help when a home's character or condition makes pricing uncertain. Divorce and bankruptcy appraisals also come up. A Standard report is usually the best fit because condition and original features vary so much from house to house, though a Desktop can work when recent interior photos are available.

Asked about Rolando

Do original details like tile and wood floors add value in Rolando?

They can, especially for buyers who want older homes with character. The appraiser looks for sales of similar homes with intact details to see how buyers have valued them. Condition matters too, since worn originals may not carry much premium.

Are Rolando homes compared with La Mesa sales?

Sometimes, since La Mesa is right across the border and has similar homes. The appraiser uses the most similar and closest sales, which may include La Mesa properties, and explains the choice in the report. The report addresses any differences between the two areas.

How do you handle a home that is very different from its neighbors?

The appraiser widens the search to find similar homes, sometimes in nearby neighborhoods, and makes adjustments for differences. You will see in the report the reasoning for picking each comparable and how each difference was treated. Unique homes may need a wider search and more careful adjustments to support the value.
