City Heights

A large mid-city area of bungalows, small multi-unit lots and canyon neighborhoods

City Heights started as its own city. East San Diego incorporated in 1912 and voted to join San Diego in 1923. Today it is one of the largest and most diverse neighborhoods in the city, stretching east from Interstate 805 toward the College Area.

City Heights is made up of many smaller neighborhoods, including Azalea Park, Hollywood Park, Teralta, Islenair, Castle, Fairmount Park, Colina Park, Cherokee Point and Swan Canyon. Each has its own feel. Values vary block to block, so we look closely at location, lot and condition.

The housing stock

City Heights has Craftsman and Spanish Revival bungalows from the 1910s to the 1940s, plus postwar stucco homes and many small apartment buildings, including Huffman six-packs from the 1960s and 1970s. Lots range from narrow city lots to larger parcels with room for ADUs. Many properties have two or more units. Condos are less common than apartments. Canyons, including Swan Canyon and Chollas Creek, shape some sub-neighborhoods. Azalea Park and Islenair have more single-family homes on quieter streets.

Value drivers to watch around City Heights

01

Sub-neighborhood location

Values move noticeably across City Heights. Azalea Park, Islenair and the quieter streets near the northern canyons often sell higher than blocks right along the busy corridors. We start with sales inside the same sub-neighborhood and widen only when we have to, so the comps reflect what a buyer on that street would compare.

02

Multi-unit lots

Many City Heights lots hold two or more units, or have room for an ADU under the city's rules. Buyers for these properties look at rental income as closely as comparable sales. We use the 2-4 unit approach when it fits and check permit records, because legal unit count drives value.

03

Busy corridors

University Avenue, El Cajon Boulevard and Fairmount Avenue bring bus rapid transit, shops and services within walking distance, along with noise and heavy traffic. Homes on quieter interior streets usually trade above homes facing the corridors. We match comps by street type as well as size.

04

Canyon and creek lots

Swan Canyon and the Chollas Creek drainage shape parts of the neighborhood. Canyon lots can bring privacy, but slope, fire hazard zoning and in low spots flood risk can offset it. We check FEMA flood maps and fire hazard maps for the subject and each comparable.

Appraisals people order here

Homes owned for decades frequently call for an estate-trust or date-of-death appraisal. Divorce appraisals come up for single-family homes and small multi-unit properties. Family-transfer appraisals help with Proposition 19 planning. Small apartment owners use 2-4 unit reports for buyouts and refinancing plans. Now and then a sponsor relies on home equity to show assets for an immigration I-864, and that calls for an appraisal. A Desktop report can work for standard homes. For multi-unit properties or homes with ADUs, a Standard report with an inspection is usually best.

Asked about City Heights

Can you use a City Heights home for an I-864 affidavit of support?

Yes. An appraisal can document the value of a sponsor's home equity for an I-864. We provide a report that shows the value and how it was reached.

How do you value a lot with a house and an ADU?

We look at sales of similar properties with and without ADUs and at rental income. Permitted units get full credit; unpermitted ones are noted and usually get less weight.

Do values differ much across City Heights?

Yes. Sub-neighborhoods like Azalea Park and Islenair often have higher values than blocks near busy corridors. We compare within the same area when possible.
